## Core logic

Participant observation is non-linear:

`broad concern ↔ setting ↔ participation ↔ observation ↔ records ↔ analysis ↔ refined question`

Its distinguishing commitments are direct involvement in ordinary settings, attention to insiders' meanings, flexible problem definition, deep case understanding, interpretive theory, and methodological pluralism.

## Working procedure

1. Confirm that the question requires access to everyday practice or insider meaning.
2. State the researcher's interests, social position, constraints, and ethical obligations.
3. Match a feasible setting to the evolving problem and negotiate legitimate access.
4. Choose a participation role deliberately; record how position enables and limits observation.
5. Build reciprocal field relationships without treating rapport as ownership or unlimited consent.
6. Begin with unfocused observation, then focus through emerging questions and negative cases.
7. Combine direct observation with informal/formal interviews, documents, artifacts, and personal experience.
8. Write records promptly and maintain descriptive, methodological, analytic, and reflexive material.
9. Analyze throughout fieldwork by coding, comparing, classifying, sequencing, and reconstructing patterns.
10. Plan a negotiated exit and write for a defined audience while protecting participants.

## Decision rules

- Prefer participant observation for exploratory, descriptive, meaning-centered questions in accessible natural settings.
- Do not use it as a substitute for experiments when the primary aim is controlled causal identification, or for probability surveys when population estimates are required.
- Treat every physical and social position as a partial viewpoint.
- Use covert participation only under exceptional, ethically defensible conditions; lack of access is not by itself sufficient justification.
- Rapport improves access but may also create over-identification, obligations, and selective vision.
- Personal experience is data and an instrument of understanding, not self-validating truth.
- Concepts should be tested in practical usage with multiple indicators and competing observations.
- Stop collecting when the analytic purpose is adequately served, not when reality is “complete.”

## Preferred output

Return: **question → setting/entrée → role and standpoint → relationship/ethics plan → evidence portfolio → record system → iterative analysis → exit/reporting plan → limitations**.
