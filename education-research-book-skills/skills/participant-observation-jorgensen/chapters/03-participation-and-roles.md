# 参与日常生活：位置与角色

## Situated observation

Physical position changes what can be seen, heard, smelled, touched, and experienced. Social position—age, gender, occupation, ethnicity, status, prior ties, institutional authority—changes what people show, say, conceal, and expect. No single position delivers the whole field.

## Role continuum

Roles range from near-complete outsider to near-complete insider. Choose a role based on the question, access, safety, ethical obligations, skills, and the kind of experience required. Reassess it as the field changes.

- An outsider role supports distance and broad comparison but may miss tacit meaning.
- An intermediate membership role balances involvement and analytic mobility.
- An insider role opens embodied and backstage experience but intensifies obligation and over-identification.
- “Becoming the phenomenon” is an extreme strategy for experiencing a world from within; it may be impossible, unsafe, deceptive, or identity-altering.

## Control the viewpoint

Use deliberate shifts of time, place, role, relationship, and sensory angle. Compare what each viewpoint makes available. Record not only what happened but why this researcher, in this position, could witness it.

## Teams and assistants

Teams provide multiple viewpoints, wider coverage, role diversity, and cross-checking. They also introduce inconsistent observation, unequal risk, coordination costs, and ownership disputes. Train together, compare records, preserve disagreements, and make local collaborators' labor and authorship visible.
