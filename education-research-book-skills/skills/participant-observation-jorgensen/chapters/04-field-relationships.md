# 建立和维持实地关系

## Trust is graded and situated

Trust and cooperation are matters of degree, not permanent achievements. Assess information by asking how a person knows, what interests they have, whether the account fits their experience, and what other observations confirm or contradict it.

## Relationship practices

- spend unhurried time in ordinary activity;
- listen without converting every exchange into extraction;
- find genuine common interests;
- disclose relevant aspects of self without manipulating intimacy;
- participate competently and reliably;
- make requests proportionate;
- clarify confidentiality and the limits of promises;
- revisit consent when the purpose or risk changes.

## Reciprocity and exchange

Field relations involve material and non-material exchange: payment, service, information, recognition, companionship, friendship, and access. Make exchange explicit enough to prevent coercion or false expectations. Friendship does not erase the asymmetry created by later analysis and publication.

## Obstacles as information

Refusal, avoidance, conflict, deception, marginalization, and gatekeeping may reveal field organization. Do not treat them merely as barriers to overcome. Also guard against “over-rapport,” where loyalty, dependency, or identification narrows judgment.

## Emotional and identity effects

Participation may produce excitement, anxiety, disillusionment, disgust, grief, or divided identity. Use confidential reflexive notes, peer debriefing, supervision, and planned recovery. Do not expose identifiable participant details while seeking support.
