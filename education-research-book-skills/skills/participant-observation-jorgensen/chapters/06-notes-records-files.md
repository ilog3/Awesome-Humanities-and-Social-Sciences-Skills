# 笔记、记录和档案

## Record promptly

Memory decays and reorganizes experience. Make jottings when feasible, then expand them as soon as possible after leaving the interaction. Treat recording as a daily research practice, not clerical cleanup.

## Maintain distinct but linked records

- **Descriptive:** people, action, dialogue, objects, place, sequence, sensory detail.
- **Methodological:** access, sampling, role decisions, changes to procedure.
- **Analytic:** questions, comparisons, codes, patterns, alternative explanations.
- **Reflexive:** emotion, identity, assumptions, relationships, possible effects on conduct.
- **Administrative:** dates, consent status, files, expenses, contact and risk actions.

Mark remembered dialogue as reconstructed; do not present it as verbatim. Distinguish observed action, participant account, researcher inference, and later interpretation.

## Media choices

Paper, audio, photography, video, and digital tools each alter attention and field relations. Obtain appropriate consent, minimize collection, encrypt sensitive files, separate identity keys, and maintain backups. Richer media do not automatically create better evidence.

## File system

Use stable identifiers and link each analytic excerpt to date, event, participant pseudonym, consent constraints, and original record. Periodically classify and duplicate the archive for analysis while preserving a read-only master. A usable archive supports retrieval without stripping context.

## Fieldnote time budget

Plan substantial writing time after observation; an hour in the field can require several hours of expansion and organization. If the schedule makes timely records impossible, reduce field exposure rather than accumulating unrecorded experience.
