# 进入研究现场

## Match problem and setting

Choose a setting that makes the phenomenon observable and is feasible within time, money, competence, language, safety, and access constraints. Informally examine several candidate sites. The setting always enables some observations and blocks others.

Characterize access along several continua:

- visible ↔ hidden;
- frontstage ↔ backstage;
- open ↔ closed;
- public ↔ private;
- routine ↔ exceptional.

## Map field politics

Identify formal gatekeepers, informal leaders, sponsors, opponents, vulnerable members, and people who can revoke practical access even when formal approval exists. Permission from an organization does not replace individual consent where it is required.

## Entry strategies

- **Open entry:** disclose the researcher role and negotiate permission. Revisit consent as participation changes.
- **Role-based entry:** take a legitimate participant role that gives ordinary access; disclose research as required.
- **Mixed entry:** different people or spaces may have different levels of disclosure; document boundaries carefully.
- **Covert entry:** creates deception, privacy, harm, and inability-to-consent risks. Use only when justified by exceptional public-interest conditions and ethical review.

## Early observation

Start broadly. Preserve the first experience of strangeness because familiarity quickly makes features invisible. Then select observations using opportunity, theoretical relevance, variation, and professional judgment. Record what access strategy itself reveals about hierarchy and boundaries.
