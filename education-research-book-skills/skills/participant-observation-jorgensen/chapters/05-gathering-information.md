# 观察和收集资料

## Progressive focusing

Begin with unfocused observation to learn the field's categories and preserve surprise. Review records, identify provisional patterns and gaps, then focus later observation. Repeat:

`observe → record → question → focus → seek variation/negative cases → revise`

## Evidence portfolio

- **Direct observation:** action, interaction, routine, spatial order, timing, breakdowns.
- **Informal conversation:** meanings in the flow of ordinary participation.
- **Formal interview:** deliberate follow-up, biographies, comparisons, and explicit accounts.
- **Questionnaire:** limited standardized coverage when it serves the field design.
- **Documents and communication:** letters, diaries, memos, rules, publicity, media, records.
- **Artifacts:** clothing, images, tools, layouts, objects, and material traces.
- **Personal experience:** embodied access to feeling and tacit practice.

## Observation protocol

Ask: who, what, when, where, with whom, through what sequence, under what rule, with what exception, and with what meaning to participants? Attend to routine and anomaly. Separate absence of observation from evidence of absence.

## Interviewing inside participation

Let observation inform interview questions and interviews redirect observation. Compare what people say with what they do without assuming either is the truth; divergence is itself a phenomenon requiring contextual explanation.

## Personal experience guardrail

Use experience to generate questions and test abstract interpretations. Triangulate it because the researcher's body, competence, history, and role are not interchangeable with those of participants.
