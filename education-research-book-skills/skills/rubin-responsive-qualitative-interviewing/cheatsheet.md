# Responsive interviewing cheatsheet

## Choose the structure

| Need | Structure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Preserve a long personal account | Floodgate | Minimizes disruptive questioning |
| Comparable coverage of known domains | Tree-and-branch | Ensures each branch is explored |
| Follow discovery but retain focus | River-and-channel | Balances responsiveness and coverage |

## Choose the question

- Need the central domain → **main question**.
- Current answer is vague, abstract, incomplete, or too fast → **probe**.
- New idea matters across interviews → **follow-up question** now or later.
- Partner makes a broad claim → ask for a concrete episode and exceptions.
- Partner reports someone else's motive → ask what was observed and how they know.
- Accounts conflict → reconstruct context before asking which is correct.

## Credibility decisions

- Missing role/viewpoint → add a knowledgeable partner.
- Same mechanism in a similar case → supports recurrence.
- Different case produces predicted contrast → clarifies boundary.
- Contrary case breaks the explanation → revise; do not discard it.
- Lots of quotations but unclear selection → improve transparency.
- Repeated codes but no causal story → build and test a mechanism.

## Interview red flags

Question contains the preferred answer; two questions are bundled; jargon is unexplained; probes repeatedly challenge; distress is treated as productive; partner cannot know the fact firsthand; guide order overrides emerging meaning; researcher promises confidentiality that the setting cannot support.

## Stop or continue?

Continue when a core concept is vague, a major perspective is missing, rivals remain untested, or new interviews change the mechanism. Consider stopping when definitions are stable, major variations and contrary cases are addressed, and new material adds detail rather than restructuring findings.

## Writing smell test

If a conclusion could have been written before the interviews, the design was likely confirmatory. If quotations can stand under any theme, context is missing. If every partner agrees, inspect sampling and relationship pressure. If confidence is uniform across claims, evidence strength has not been calibrated.
