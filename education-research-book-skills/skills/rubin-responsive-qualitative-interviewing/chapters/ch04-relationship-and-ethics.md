# Chapter 5: 谈话伙伴关系

## Core idea

Interview quality and ethics depend on the relationship. The interviewer's personality, emotion, identity, disclosures, and social position affect trust, topics, interpretation, and risk.

## Researcher self-management

Anticipate anxiety, fatigue, emotional contagion, over-identification, defensiveness, and impatience. Know whether your style is highly structured, conflict-avoidant, talkative, or reserved, and compensate deliberately. Keep reflexive memos on how these traits affected the conversation.

## Role and boundaries

Clarify whether you are researcher, evaluator, teacher, colleague, advocate, insider, outsider, or several at once. Crossing class, racial, gender, occupational, institutional, or linguistic boundaries can expand understanding and create misunderstanding or danger. Ask partners how they perceive your role.

## Reciprocity and self-disclosure

Share enough to be human, clarify purpose, and support mutual understanding. Do not trade intimacy for data, promise outcomes outside your control, or let disclosure shift the burden of care onto the partner.

## Building trust

Make respectful initial contact, explain why this person was approached, provide realistic time and topic information, make participation voluntary, keep appointments, listen closely, and honor later restrictions.

## Ethical responsibility

Formal review is a floor. Address consent as ongoing; confidentiality and deductive identification; emotional distress; coercive organizational relationships; third parties named in stories; data storage; quotation and future reuse; and consequences of publication.

## Worked example

When a teacher interviews her own students, declining may feel academically risky. Recruitment should be separated from grading, consent should be age-appropriate, participation must not change treatment, sensitive responses need a safeguarding plan, and quotations must be checked for recognizability.

## Key takeaways

1. Relationship is part of the method, not contamination around it.
2. Trust does not eliminate verification or consent boundaries.
3. Monitor how the study changes both partners and researcher.
