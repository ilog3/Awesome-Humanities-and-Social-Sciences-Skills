# Chapter 12: 呈现研究结果

## Core idea

Reporting should engage a specific audience, show the evidence behind conclusions, and be transparent enough that readers can judge credibility without drowning in raw detail.

## Choose medium, style, and tone

Results may appear as article, thesis, report, briefing, presentation, or public communication. Match vocabulary, length, recommendation level, and methodological detail to the audience while preserving analytic integrity.

Use a tone of calibrated confidence: strong where firsthand converging evidence is rich, conditional where cases differ, and explicit where knowledge is incomplete.

## Writing workflow

1. Write a focused summary of the central answer, mechanism, and boundaries.
2. Build an outline that moves from question to contextualized findings and implications.
3. Draft evidence-rich analysis sections.
4. Add introduction, literature, methods, conclusion, and limitations in the order that helps completion.
5. Revise for logical progression, evidence sufficiency, voice, confidentiality, and audience needs.
6. Obtain feedback from knowledgeable readers and, where appropriate, conversational partners.

## Evidence use

For each major claim show credible primary evidence, variation, and contrary material. Introduce quotations with source position and context; analyze rather than let them stand alone. Paraphrases should not conceal uncertainty or secondhand knowledge.

## Worked example

A findings section begins with the claim that budget pressure changes information practice, reconstructs a decision episode from multiple roles, presents contrasting quotations, explains the mechanism, identifies a case where it did not occur, and states the conditions under which the conclusion may apply.

## Credibility checklist

- Does the conclusion answer the stated question?
- Can readers see partner selection and question evolution?
- Is every major claim supported by traceable evidence?
- Are alternative views and design flaws discussed?
- Are interpretations limited to what partners could know?
- Can quotations reveal identities despite pseudonyms?

## Key takeaways

1. Writing continues analysis.
2. Evidence should permit judgment, not merely decorate conclusions.
3. Methodological honesty increases rather than weakens persuasiveness.
