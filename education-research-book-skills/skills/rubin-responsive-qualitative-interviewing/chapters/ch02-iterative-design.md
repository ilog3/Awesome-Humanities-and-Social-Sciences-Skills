# Chapter 3: 迭代研究设计

## Core idea

Design anticipates analysis and evolves with discovery. Researchers plan enough structure to answer a significant question while reserving capacity to pursue concepts that could not be known in advance.

## From topic to question

Choose a topic from experience, literature, policy debate, puzzling observation, or community concern. Narrow it into a question that asks how people understand, experience, produce, or respond to something. Confirm that the question needs depth and context rather than standardized distribution alone.

## Feasibility test

Assess access to knowledgeable partners, emotional and political sensitivity, researcher competence, language, time for transcription and analysis, travel, recording, data security, and the likelihood of obtaining detailed firsthand accounts.

## Anticipate analysis, not findings

Before interviewing, specify the **type** of result sought:

- a detailed description;
- contrasting understandings;
- a process or sequence;
- an explanatory mechanism;
- elaboration or revision of theory;
- conditions under which a conclusion travels.

Do not predict the substantive answer and then recruit only confirming voices.

## Iterative design loop

After each interview:

1. summarize what was learned;
2. define new or unclear concepts;
3. identify missing perspectives and contrary cases;
4. revise main or follow-up questions;
5. select the next partner or setting strategically;
6. record why the design changed.

## Worked example

Initial question: “How do adult students experience university support?” Early interviews introduce “being treated as a real student.” Later sampling deliberately includes evening staff, conventional-age students, and adults who left. Follow-up questions examine recognition practices, shifting the expected result from service satisfaction to a mechanism of institutional belonging.

## Key takeaways

1. Analysis starts before the first question is finalized.
2. Flexibility needs a documented rationale.
3. The target is a credible type of explanation, not a foreknown conclusion.
