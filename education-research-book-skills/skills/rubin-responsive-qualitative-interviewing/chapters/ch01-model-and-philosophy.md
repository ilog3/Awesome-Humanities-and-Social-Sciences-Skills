# Chapters 1–2: 模式、用途与哲学

## Core idea

Responsive interviewing seeks rich, credible understandings of how people interpret and experience their worlds. Meaning is constructed in natural social contexts and further shaped in the interview relationship.

## Uses

Apply qualitative interviews to investigative inquiry, explanatory case study, ethnographic interpretation, theory elaboration, oral and life history, organizational culture, evaluation, and action research. Clarify whether the interview is the primary method or one evidence stream in a larger design.

## Two ideal types

- **Cultural interview:** explores shared meanings, rules, interpretations, and taken-for-granted understandings.
- **Topic interview:** explores a particular event, program, decision, process, or problem.

They are analytical poles, not mutually exclusive formats.

## Philosophical orientation

The model contrasts positivist standardization with naturalistic-interpretive constructionism while learning selectively from critical, feminist, and postmodern concerns. Reality is understood through situated interpretations; researchers are participants in knowledge production rather than invisible measuring instruments.

This does not mean all claims are equally persuasive. Judge them by firsthand knowledge, specificity, internal coherence, comparison, contrary evidence, and transparent reasoning.

## Worked example

To study a school discipline policy, a topic interview asks how one incident unfolded and how a decision was made. A cultural interview asks what “respect,” “order,” and “fairness” mean among teachers and students. Combining them reveals both the event and the interpretive system that made actions sensible.

## Anti-patterns

- Standardizing every wording when responsive clarification is necessary.
- Treating partners' accounts as either objective facts or mere subjective opinions.
- Claiming neutrality while hiding the researcher's role and commitments.

## Key takeaways

1. Select interviewing when understanding from another's standpoint is central.
2. Preserve natural context and competing interpretations.
3. Make researcher influence examinable rather than pretending it disappears.
