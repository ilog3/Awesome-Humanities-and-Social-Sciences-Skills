# Chapter 6: 作为拓展谈话的访谈

## Core idea

A responsive interview resembles an extended everyday conversation in coherence, clarification, narrative, and mutual understanding, but differs in purpose, preparation, asymmetry, recording, ethical duty, and analytic use.

## Interview phases

1. **Open:** introduce yourself, purpose, recording, confidentiality, expected topics, and right to stop.
2. **Ease in:** begin with accessible descriptive questions and show empathic understanding.
3. **Develop:** pursue stories, examples, sequences, meanings, distinctions, and context.
4. **Sharpen:** after sufficient trust, ask about contradiction, conflict, sensitive issues, or rival explanations without accusation.
5. **Regulate emotion:** pause, acknowledge, offer choice, and never treat distress as desirable depth.
6. **Close:** summarize, ask what was missed, invite questions, explain next steps, confirm contact and quotation arrangements.
7. **Review:** assess depth, clarity, vividness, nuance, coverage, relation, and new analytic leads.

## Keeping coherence

Signal topic shifts, connect a probe to what was just said, periodically summarize, and return gently after useful digressions. A coherent interview need not follow the guide's order.

## Worked example

Instead of abruptly asking “Why did the program fail?”, begin with the partner's role and a typical day, reconstruct a particular turning point, clarify who decided what, then ask how the partner understands failure and how others might disagree. Close by checking the reconstructed sequence.

## Variations

Group interviews add interaction but dilute confidentiality and individual depth. Telephone or remote interviews increase reach and reduce contextual cues; establish identity, privacy, connection contingency, and a clear verbal structure.

## Key takeaways

1. Guide the interaction without scripting the answer.
2. Earn sharp questions through clarity and relationship.
3. End with ethical and analytic closure, not simply a stopped recorder.
