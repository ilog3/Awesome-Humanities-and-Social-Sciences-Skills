# Chapter 4: 选择谈话伙伴并建立可信度

## Core idea

Credible conclusions depend on choosing people who know the issue from relevant positions and on showing that evidence is thorough, accurate, and transparently produced.

## Select partners

Seek firsthand experience or specialized knowledge, willingness and ability to explain, variation in position, and access to distinct parts of the process. Begin purposively and revise sampling as concepts emerge.

Use:

- **similarity sampling** to test whether an interpretation recurs under comparable conditions;
- **difference sampling** to identify boundary conditions and broaden or restrict claims;
- **contrary-case sampling** to challenge an emerging conclusion;
- **role-chain sampling** to follow a process across decision makers, implementers, recipients, and observers.

## Credibility framework

### Thoroughness

Cover relevant viewpoints, contexts, stages, details, and plausible alternatives. Thorough does not mean interviewing everyone.

### Accuracy

Ask how partners know, seek concrete examples, distinguish direct and secondhand knowledge, rephrase understanding for correction, and compare accounts with other evidence.

### Transparency

Document recruitment, refusals, relationships, question evolution, transcription, coding, interpretation, and limits. Let readers see how evidence supports each main conclusion.

## Worked example

A study of a prison disturbance should not rely only on administrators. It may include officers, incarcerated people, medical staff, investigators, and records. A second facility with similar conditions can test recurrence; a facility without disturbance can challenge the initial explanation.

## Stopping rule

Stop when central concepts are well defined, important variants and contrary views have been examined, new interviews mainly confirm or nuance the explanation, and remaining gaps are stated. Do not use a universal sample number.

## Key takeaways

1. Knowledge position matters more than demographic quota alone.
2. Similar and different cases calibrate the range of a conclusion.
3. Credibility is designed and demonstrated, not asserted by vocabulary.
