# Chapters 10–11: 从文本到理论

## Core idea

Analysis is systematic, iterative examination—not intuition, memory, or quotation counting. Code interactions in relation to the research purpose, then combine material differently to describe, compare, explain, and theorize.

## Prepare the material

Transcribe at the detail level needed by the question. Preserve speaker turns, uncertainty, emphases or pauses when consequential, and links to audio. After each interview write a summary and memo of concepts, themes, puzzles, gaps, and possible follow-ups.

## Concepts and themes

- A **concept** is a named idea that helps explain what partners experience or do.
- A **theme** is a broader assertion or patterned account running through material.

Find them in literature and interviews, but define them from actual usage. Refine vague labels into inclusion/exclusion criteria, properties, variation, and examples.

## Coding and first-stage analysis

Code segments, compare similar and different uses, write definitions, preserve context, and revise the code system. Grounded-theory techniques may support open discovery and constant comparison, but use them consistently rather than claiming a full methodology after generic coding.

## Analyze coded material

Classify and summarize; rank or order where evidence warrants; compare groups, times, and settings; weigh strength and directness; integrate related concepts; check against original interviews; and revise conclusions.

## Build theory

1. Reflect on how question wording shaped accounts.
2. Identify concepts/themes that co-occur or follow one another.
3. compare with existing literature;
4. propose a mechanism linking conditions, actions, and consequences;
5. specify exceptions and boundary conditions;
6. test in similar and contrasting settings;
7. develop a middle-range explanation rather than an unlimited law.

## Worked example

Finding: adult students report “not being real students.” Analysis separates recognition by staff, timetable access, peer belonging, and eligibility rules; tests their sequence; identifies cases with strong belonging despite restricted services; and revises the mechanism to institutional recognition plus peer incorporation rather than age alone.

## Key takeaways

1. Return constantly from code to interaction.
2. Mechanisms make conclusions persuasive beyond description.
3. Generalize by specifying conditions, similarity, difference, and evidence—not by sample size rhetoric.
