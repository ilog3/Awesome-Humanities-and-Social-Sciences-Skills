# Patterns

## Responsive design loop

**When to use**: Throughout an interview project.

**How**: Interview → summarize → code/memo → identify concepts, gaps, and rivals → revise questions → select next partner → document the revision.

**Trade-off**: Increases relevance but reduces identical-question comparability; retain a stable core of main questions where comparison matters.

## Knowledge-position sampling

**When to use**: Selecting conversational partners.

**How**: Map the process and roles; recruit people with firsthand access at each point; add similar cases, different cases, and potential disconfirmers as analysis develops.

**Trade-off**: Relevance improves while representativeness remains analytic, not statistical.

## Main–follow-up–probe architecture

**When to use**: Building an interview guide.

**How**: Define 4–7 main domains; prepare likely depth/detail/clarity probes; leave a section for follow-up concepts from prior interviews; end with omissions and partner questions.

**Trade-off**: Too many main questions reduce narrative depth.

## Concept refinement card

**When to use**: A recurring partner term appears important.

**How**: Record exact term, speaker/context, initial meaning, examples, nonexamples, properties, variations, related concepts, contrary usage, follow-up question, and revised definition.

**Trade-off**: A neat definition can erase contested meaning; retain variant uses.

## Mechanism-building chain

**When to use**: Moving beyond description.

**How**: Identify starting conditions → actors' interpretations → actions/interactions → intermediate changes → outcomes → exceptions → alternative mechanisms → settings where the chain should and should not recur.

**Trade-off**: Interview accounts of causation need corroboration and temporal evidence.

## Credible-claim block

**When to use**: Drafting each central finding.

**How**: State claim → provide firsthand episode → add variation → show contrary view → explain mechanism → identify evidence limits → bound transfer.

**Trade-off**: Strong reporting is longer; prioritize the claims that answer the research question.
