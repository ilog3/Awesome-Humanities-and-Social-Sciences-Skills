# 《质性访谈方法：聆听与提问的艺术》

**Authors**: Herbert J. Rubin and Irene S. Rubin | **Pages**: ~266 | **Chapters**: 12 | **Generated**: 2026-09-05

## Core framework: responsive interviewing

Responsive interviewing is a flexible, iterative conversation in which researchers select people with firsthand knowledge, listen for meaning and detail, and modify later questions and sampling in response to what they learn.

Its central loop is:

`design → interview → early analysis → new concepts/gaps → follow-up questions and sampling → refined interpretation`

### Conversational partners

Treat interviewees as conversational partners rather than objects or standardized respondents. The relationship shapes what can be asked, understood, and ethically used. Partnership does not erase differences in power, interest, or interpretive authority.

### Three question types

1. **Main questions** address the overall research question and give the interview structure.
2. **Follow-up questions** pursue concepts, themes, contradictions, and discoveries emerging across interviews.
3. **Probes** keep the current conversation clear, detailed, vivid, nuanced, and on topic.

### Credibility through thoroughness, accuracy, and transparency

- **Thoroughness:** seek relevant viewpoints, variation, detail, and disconfirming cases.
- **Accuracy:** ask how partners know, obtain firsthand accounts, compare sources, and clarify meaning.
- **Transparency:** show how people were selected, questions evolved, evidence was interpreted, and conclusions were bounded.

## Working procedure

1. Choose a meaningful, feasible topic suited to learning people's interpretations, experience, or firsthand knowledge.
2. Anticipate the type of conclusion and theory contribution—not the conclusion's content.
3. Select conversational partners for relevance, range, and later comparison.
4. Establish ethical, reciprocal relations and disclose researcher standpoint.
5. Prepare a flexible structure of main questions and expected probes.
6. Begin gently, develop depth, introduce sharp questions after rapport, regulate emotion, close responsibly, and preserve contact where promised.
7. After every interview, summarize, memo, identify missing detail and emerging ideas, and prepare follow-up questions.
8. Transcribe and code systematically; refine concepts and themes by sorting, comparing, weighing, integrating, checking, and revising.
9. Build an explanatory mechanism and state the range of situations to which it may apply.
10. Write with traceable evidence, contrary views, methodological transparency, and calibrated confidence.

## Decision rules

- Use cultural interviews to learn shared meanings and topic interviews to understand a bounded issue; many projects combine both.
- If you need standardized prevalence estimates, responsive interviewing alone is insufficient.
- Ask partners about what they experienced or know directly; treat secondhand inference as such.
- Do not follow the guide so rigidly that a new concept is lost, or so loosely that the research question disappears.
- A vivid quotation is evidence only when its source position and relevance are explained.
- Stop when additional interviews confirm and refine rather than materially change central findings, and key contrasts have been examined—not at a mechanical number.
- Software assists retrieval and organization; it does not define concepts or theory.
