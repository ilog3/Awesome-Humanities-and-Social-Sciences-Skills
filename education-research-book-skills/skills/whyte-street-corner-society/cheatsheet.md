# Field Decision Cheatsheet

| Situation | Do | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Site looks disorganized | Map repeated interaction and obligation | Outsider categories may miss informal structure |
| One person offers entry | Accept cautiously; log opened and closed networks | Sponsorship both enables and filters |
| People ask who you are | Give a truthful, proportionate explanation | Access is ongoing, not settled once |
| You want to fit in | Participate without performing false sameness | Recognizable difference can sustain trust |
| Leadership reports conflict | Observe several consequential decisions | Leadership may be domain-specific |
| A dramatic event occurs | Capture sequence and later consequences | Structure appears through action over time |
| You are asked for help | Run an intervention audit | Refusal and assistance both alter relations |
| A pattern emerges | Seek another group and a negative case | Comparison prevents sponsor-centered theory |
| Draft interpretation is ready | Invite specific feedback and record disagreement | Collaboration improves evidence without erasing authorship |
| Leaving the site | Clarify contact, records, promises, and return | Field relationships outlast data collection |

## Fast checks

- Whose network am I currently seeing?
- Which actor converts talk into collective action?
- What institution or broker connects this event outward?
- What did my presence or assistance change?
- Which participant category have I mistaken for analysis?
- What event would disconfirm my relational claim?
- What present ethical standard differs from the historical study?
