# Patterns

## Access map

**When to use:** Before and during entry.

**How:** List gatekeepers, sponsors, interpreters, factions, marginal actors, opened settings, blocked settings, obligations, and alternative routes. Update after every consequential introduction.

**Trade-off:** Strong sponsorship accelerates access but can align the researcher with one faction.

## Interaction-to-rank matrix

**When to use:** When formal titles and reported leadership conflict.

**How:** Rows are events; columns record initiator, supporters, vetoes, broker, final decision, implementation, and outcome. Look for repeated influence across situations.

**Trade-off:** Observable influence may differ by domain; avoid producing one global rank.

## Event-based fieldnote

**When to use:** After meetings, games, disputes, campaigns, or routine gatherings.

**How:** Record context → participants → sequence → direct wording → audience response → decision → consequences → participant interpretations → researcher inference → next test.

**Trade-off:** High detail limits breadth; sample events purposefully.

## Intervention audit

**When to use:** Whenever the researcher helps, advises, pays, participates politically, or influences an organization.

**How:** Document request, decision, beneficiaries, risks, field effect, ethical basis, participant response, and follow-up.

**Trade-off:** Nonintervention is also consequential; the goal is accountable judgment, not impossible neutrality.

## Worked application: school peer group

Observe recurring informal groups across classes, breaks, clubs, and online coordination. Use concrete decisions to map influence; compare student and teacher accounts; trace brokers to formal school structures; inspect how mobility opportunities change loyalties. Do not label the group as disorganized merely because it lacks official roles.
