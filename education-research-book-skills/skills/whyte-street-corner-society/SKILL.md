# Whyte's Street Corner Society

Use sustained participation and interactional evidence to discover how an apparently disorganized neighborhood is organized through informal groups, rank, loyalty, brokerage, and political obligation.

## Core models

- **Organization inside apparent disorder:** Do not infer anomie from an outsider's inability to see structure. Observe recurring interaction, influence, deference, coordination, and obligation.
- **Group before isolated individual:** Interpret behavior through a person's position in a network and the group's changing situation, not personality alone.
- **Interaction frequency and rank:** Map who gathers around whom, whose proposals become group action, who mediates disputes, and how performance tracks status.
- **Corner boys and college boys:** Treat the contrast as different patterns of group integration, aspiration, institutional connection, and mobility—not simply ability or virtue.
- **Key-person entry:** A trusted central participant can make access intelligible and teach local rules, but also filters the field. Expand beyond that person's network and record the dependency.
- **Reciprocal collaboration:** Field relationships work through mutual contribution, yet reciprocity does not erase unequal authorship, rewards, risk, or retrospective disagreement.

## Workflow

1. **Question the outsider diagnosis.** State what looks disorganized and what repeated relations might reveal. Read [chapters/ch01-cornerville-and-field-of-relations.md](chapters/ch01-cornerville-and-field-of-relations.md).
2. **Map natural groups.** Track membership, rank, activities, leadership, factions, loyalty, and change across settings. Read [chapters/ch02-corner-gangs-status-and-mobility.md](chapters/ch02-corner-gangs-status-and-mobility.md).
3. **Follow brokerage outward.** Trace how clubs, gambling, police, ward politics, jobs, and civic organizations connect local groups to formal institutions. Read [chapters/ch03-rackets-politics-and-obligation.md](chapters/ch03-rackets-politics-and-obligation.md).
4. **Enter through relationships.** Identify sponsors, gatekeepers, interpreters, peripheral actors, and excluded people; disclose the project proportionately and learn local conduct. Read [chapters/ch04-entry-key-informants-and-participation.md](chapters/ch04-entry-key-informants-and-participation.md).
5. **Learn by participating without imitation.** Join ordinary activities, make mistakes repairably, and preserve a recognizable researcher identity.
6. **Record interaction, not impressions.** Capture sequences, speakers, audiences, decisions, movements, and outcomes promptly. Read [chapters/ch05-fieldnotes-analysis-and-redesign.md](chapters/ch05-fieldnotes-analysis-and-redesign.md).
7. **Redesign iteratively.** Let observed structures alter the original plan; use focused observations, comparison, simple relational diagrams, documents, and selective counts to test claims.
8. **Audit intervention and reciprocity.** Treat advice, job help, political participation, money, friendship, and manuscript feedback as data and ethical commitments. Read [chapters/ch06-ethics-reflexivity-and-afterlife.md](chapters/ch06-ethics-reflexivity-and-afterlife.md).
9. **Bound conclusions historically.** Distinguish Cornerville findings, reusable fieldwork heuristics, and later ethical standards.

## Required outputs

For a study plan provide: outsider assumption, site and historical context, access map, role and disclosure plan, group/network sampling, event-focused observation protocol, fieldnote and analysis cycle, intervention boundaries, reciprocity plan, ethics and anonymity, validation strategy, exit, participant feedback, and limits.

For analysis use [patterns.md](patterns.md); for terminology use [glossary.md](glossary.md); for field decisions use [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md).

## Guardrails

- Do not romanticize “community” or erase conflict, exclusion, gendered access, crime, racism, and institutional power.
- Do not let a key informant stand for the whole field.
- Do not read rank from one dramatic event; look for stable patterns across situations.
- Do not convert the corner-boy/college-boy contrast into fixed types applicable everywhere.
- Do not assume immersion cancels observer effects or that friendship makes extraction harmless.
- Do not copy Whyte's historical disclosure and consent practices into a current study without current ethics review.
- Do not treat later defenses or criticisms as final; report the contested methodological afterlife.
