## Core model

Learning is a threefold dialogic practice:

1. Dialogue with the **object/world** — cognitive practice.
2. Dialogue with **other learners and teachers** — relational practice.
3. Dialogue with a **new self** — reflective, identity-forming practice.

Organize learning as **active, collaborative, and reflective**, and organize school reform at three linked levels:

`students learn together ↔ teachers learn together ↔ parents/citizens participate and learn together`

## Working procedure

1. Observe what students are actually doing, thinking, saying, and not saying.
2. Replace generic labels with concrete episodes and named learning difficulties.
3. Diagnose whether activity is genuine inquiry or a performance of “subjectivity.”
4. Redesign teacher response around listening, connecting voices, materials, and unresolved questions.
5. Open ordinary lessons to colleagues; use lesson evidence rather than judging teacher personality.
6. Remove meetings and administrative routines that crowd out lesson study and curriculum work.
7. Involve families as participants in learning, with low barriers to entry.
8. Iterate slowly; evaluate changes in relationships and learning quality, not event visibility.

## Decision rules

- Prefer a quiet classroom of mutual listening over loud, rapid-fire participation.
- Treat hesitant, ambiguous speech as possible thinking, not deficiency.
- Student agency emerges in relationships with teachers, peers, materials, and environment; it is not isolated self-direction.
- Open daily lessons, not specially staged showcase lessons.
- Every teacher, including leaders, should expose practice to collegial inquiry.
- Curriculum is the accumulated experience and trajectory of learning, not merely objectives or a timetable.
- Begin integrated learning with a real theme worth investigating, not with decorative activities.
- Reform starts inside classrooms but needs organizational, family, community, and administrative support.

## Evidence discipline

The book develops arguments through long-term classroom observation and Japanese school cases. Treat them as design knowledge and mechanism-rich examples, not universal effect estimates. Adapt for local class size, policy, safeguarding, labor rules, and community conditions.

## Preferred output

Return: **observed episode → learning problem → relationship/mechanism → smallest redesign → collegial evidence plan → organizational support → review point**.
