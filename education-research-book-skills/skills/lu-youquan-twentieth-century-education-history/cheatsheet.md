# Twentieth-Century Education History Cheatsheet

## Fast analysis

1. What is the exact place, period, level, and reform or idea?
2. What did historical actors say, and what actually happened?
3. How does the book interpret it?
4. Which political, economic, technological, cultural, demographic, and international conditions mattered?
5. Who defined the educational problem and who was excluded?
6. What thought tradition shaped purpose, curriculum, method, and authority?
7. Through what institutions and resources did reform operate?
8. How did teachers, learners, families, and local actors adapt or resist it?
9. Who benefited, who paid, and what harm or coercion occurred?
10. Which evidence, rival explanation, and present-day update are needed?

## Common errors

| Error | Repair |
|---|---|
| Ideas caused reform | Add actors, coalitions, institutions, resources, and mechanism. |
| A law changed education | Trace enactment and experience. |
| One country represents a bloc | Analyze internal and regional variation. |
| Similar labels mean similar reforms | Compare function and context. |
| Later means better | Evaluate purpose, evidence, distribution, and harm. |
| Context excuses coercion | Explain historically and judge explicitly. |
| Current crisis repeats history | Test analogy and non-analogy. |
| Official account equals history | Triangulate with primary, local, and affected-group sources. |

## Minimal output

- Scope and periodization
- Evidence-layer ledger
- Context and problem definition
- Thought/reform mechanism
- Chronology and actors
- Implementation and experience
- Comparison and rival explanation
- Distribution, coercion, and unintended effects
- Continuity/rupture and present relevance
- Verification list

## Historical boundary

The 2012 edition principally interprets twentieth-century developments through the 1990s and includes strong authorial and ideological judgments. Use current historiography and primary sources before making precise factual, causal, or contemporary claims.
