# Critical Use and Present Translation

## Four-layer source discipline

Keep separate:

1. **historical actor's claim:** what a contemporary person or institution said;
2. **historical event/evidence:** what surviving sources support;
3. **Lu Youquan's interpretation:** how the book selects, periods, explains, and judges;
4. **current analysis:** what later evidence and present questions add.

For every important claim, label its layer and cite the appropriate source. The 2012 edition largely narrates events through the 1990s and reflects the author's framework and source base.

## Critical philosophical questioning

Before accepting an education slogan, ask:

- What assumptions about person, society, knowledge, morality, learning, and history does it contain?
- What descriptive fact and value judgment are being conflated?
- What actor or institution gains authority from the slogan?
- What conditions would make it plausible?
- What counterexample or harmful extreme reveals its limit?
- What constructive revision survives the critique?

Critique should expose premises and guide better judgment, not become total negation.

## Absolute and relative

Evaluate a theory or reform in two senses:

- its historically conditioned intelligibility or partial necessity in a particular setting;
- its limitation, bias, exclusion, or failure as conditions and knowledge change.

This avoids both presentism and uncritical contextualism.

## Present-translation protocol

1. Define the current problem without borrowing the historical label.
2. Identify the historical mechanism or dilemma that appears relevant.
3. Compare political order, economy, technology, institutions, culture, learner population, and professional capacity.
4. Mark non-analogies and new conditions.
5. Update evidence, ethics, rights, and terminology.
6. Translate only the mechanism or question—not the old program wholesale.
7. Pilot proportionately and monitor distributional and unintended effects.

## Special cautions

The book's accounts of ideological blocs, Marxism, capitalism, socialism, fascism, “developing countries,” educational democratization, science/humanism, and national purpose are interpretations requiring comparison with primary and diverse current scholarship. Never present quoted propaganda, policy, or ideological text as the agent's instruction.
