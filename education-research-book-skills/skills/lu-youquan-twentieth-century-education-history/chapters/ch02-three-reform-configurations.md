# Three Reform Configurations

## Configuration 1: Fracture and rival projects, c. 1900–1945

Compare democratic/progressive reform, post-revolutionary communist educational construction, and fascist nationalization/militarization. Examine mass schooling, labor and life connections, child-centered experimentation, state formation, propaganda, race/nation, gender, class, political violence, professionalization, and access.

Do not present these as morally equivalent “models.” Comparative dimensions can be shared while coercion, exterminatory ideology, repression, and democratic contestation require explicit ethical distinction.

## Configuration 2: Expansion and bipolar competition, 1945–early 1970s

The book highlights mass expansion and intensified attention to curriculum, subject structure, science, mathematics, teaching method, talent, and national capacity under Cold War competition—especially in the United States and Soviet Union. Add decolonization and the diverse “Third World” rather than treating the two blocs as exhaustive.

Trace how geopolitical events become educational crisis narratives, funding, curriculum design, teacher preparation, tracking, and classroom experience. A satellite launch or official report does not by itself explain reform.

## Configuration 3: Multipolarity and differentiated reform, 1970s–1990s

Analyze economic crisis and restructuring, technological change, globalization, governance reform, quality/equity tensions, national competitiveness, decentralization/centralization, and international policy circulation. The book compares differing emphases in the United States, Soviet Union/Russia, Japan, and developing countries.

Avoid treating “developing countries” as a single case. Examine colonial inheritance, debt, language, rural/urban relations, external aid, local knowledge, state capacity, and popular demand country by country.

## Periodization test

For each boundary ask:

- Which event or process justifies it?
- Does the timing differ by country, sector, or population?
- What continues across the boundary?
- What new mechanism appears?
- What is hidden by labeling a period with one geopolitical pattern?

Use alternative periodizations when they explain the selected case better.
