# Comparative Reform Analysis

## Within-case reconstruction

Build chronology before comparison:

1. inherited institutional and intellectual conditions;
2. economic, political, technological, cultural, military, and demographic pressures;
3. crisis narrative and agenda setters;
4. goals, values, target populations, and governing coalition;
5. legal, financial, curricular, organizational, and pedagogical instruments;
6. central, local, professional, family, student, and external responses;
7. implementation, resistance, adaptation, and uneven access;
8. immediate outputs, lived effects, delayed outcomes, and unintended consequences;
9. revision, reversal, sedimentation, and later memory.

## Context–mechanism–outcome

Replace national-character explanations with mechanisms. Examples include fiscal capacity, examination incentives, teacher preparation, bureaucratic coordination, political repression, labor demand, language policy, curriculum expertise, family strategy, and international policy borrowing.

For each causal claim specify context, mechanism, actors, outcome, evidence, temporal sequence, and rival explanation.

## Success test

Never report success without naming:

- success for whom and by whose values;
- baseline and counterfactual;
- intended and unintended effects;
- distribution by class, gender, race/ethnicity, region, language, disability, or other relevant position;
- durability and time horizon;
- evidence quality and missing voices.

Expansion, standardization, centralization, decentralization, vocationalization, comprehensivization, or pedagogical innovation can each solve one problem while creating another.
