# 理论提升与研究报告

## From material to theory

Move in traceable steps:

`episode/excerpt → code → category → relationship/pattern → explanatory proposition → boundary/exception`

Compare within and across cases, ask what conditions produce a pattern, test rival explanations, and retain negative cases. Theory can be modest and local: a clear mechanism or conceptual distinction is preferable to unsupported grand language.

## Report elements

1. title and concise abstract;
2. practical context and research problem;
3. relevant conceptual background;
4. question and qualitative design;
5. participants, setting, role, ethics, and procedures;
6. data organization and analysis trail;
7. findings organized by analytic themes or process;
8. evidence-rich interpretation and theoretical development;
9. implications for educational practice;
10. limitations, transfer conditions, references, and necessary appendices.

## Writing process

Draft a provisional outline, write the most evidence-rich analytic section first if helpful, then iterate between findings and framing. Use participant quotations and scenes selectively; introduce context and analyze their significance. Keep claims proportional to evidence and separate participants' voices from the author's interpretation.

## Style

Write clearly enough for practitioners while retaining conceptual precision. Avoid excessive terminology, ornamental quotation, moral judgment disguised as finding, and chronological data dumps. A report is persuasive when the reader can follow how evidence supports interpretation, not when certainty is overstated.
