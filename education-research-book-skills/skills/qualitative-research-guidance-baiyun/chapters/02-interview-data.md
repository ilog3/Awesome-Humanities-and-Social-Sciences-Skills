# 访谈资料的收集与分析

## Prepare

Define what the interview can reveal that observation or documents cannot. Select participants purposively, negotiate a safe setting, prepare open topics and follow-up probes, test recording, plan consent and confidentiality, and disclose the researcher's dual role where relevant.

## Conduct

- begin with accessible experience and concrete episodes;
- ask one open question at a time;
- follow participants' words, sequence, and concerns;
- probe “what happened next,” examples, contrasts, feelings, meaning, and change;
- tolerate silence and avoid answering for the participant;
- check understanding without demanding agreement;
- write contextual and reflexive notes immediately afterward.

## Focus groups

Use focus groups to study interaction, shared vocabularies, disagreement, and collective norms. Compose groups with attention to hierarchy and safety. The moderator balances participation without converting the session into serial individual interviews. Explain that researchers cannot guarantee what other participants will repeat outside the group.

## Life-history interviews

Build a temporal map of turning points, relationships, institutions, transitions, and remembered contexts. Treat the life story as a present act of narration as well as an account of the past. Compare across episodes and other records; do not diagnose memory difference as dishonesty by default.

## Recording and organization

Choose audio, notes, or layered records based on purpose and consent. Transcribe consequential detail and document normalization, omissions, pseudonyms, and uncertainty. Code iteratively, group related codes, compare participants and negative cases, and link every interpretation to the source segment.

## Interviewing students

Reduce authority pressure: make nonparticipation consequence-free, use age-appropriate language, avoid interviewing about sensitive matters in unsafe visibility, permit breaks and withdrawal, and separate research data from grading or discipline. Use drawings, timelines, objects, or activity-based prompts only when they serve the question and are not coercive tests.
