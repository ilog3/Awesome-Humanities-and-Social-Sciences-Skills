# 质的研究基础与设计

## What qualitative research does

Qualitative research studies meaning, process, experience, interaction, and context in natural or minimally manipulated settings. The researcher interacts with participants, uses multiple forms of evidence, reasons largely inductively, and produces an interpretive account rather than reducing the phenomenon to variables alone.

It is especially useful when existing categories are inadequate, a process is poorly understood, different actors define the same event differently, or a school problem must be understood before action is chosen.

## Historical and epistemic orientation

The field developed through anthropology, sociology, education, phenomenology, hermeneutics, interactionism, critical inquiry, and other traditions. Do not combine incompatible assumptions casually. State what counts as knowledge in the chosen approach and how the researcher-participant relationship affects it.

## Design questions

- What practical and scholarly problem is being addressed?
- Why is a qualitative design appropriate?
- What is the bounded case, setting, process, or experience?
- Who can illuminate variation, typicality, and contradiction?
- What is the researcher's role and prior relationship?
- Which data forms answer which subquestions?
- How will collection and analysis inform one another?
- What would make the account credible and useful?

## Common forms

- **Case study:** an in-depth inquiry into a bounded educational case.
- **Ethnographic inquiry:** patterned culture and everyday practice in a group.
- **Narrative/life history:** experience organized across biographical time.
- **Action-oriented inquiry:** iterative understanding and change within practice.
- **Grounded/theory-building inquiry:** concepts and relationships developed through systematic comparison.

Name the form only when its logic actually governs sampling, evidence, analysis, and writing.
