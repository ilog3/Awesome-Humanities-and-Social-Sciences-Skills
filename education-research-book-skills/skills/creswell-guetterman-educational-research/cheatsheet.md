# Educational Research Cheatsheet

## Design in one minute

1. What evidence is deficient and who needs it?
2. What purpose and exact question?
3. Variables or central phenomenon?
4. Which of eight designs has the defining features needed?
5. Who/what is sampled, assigned, observed, or narrated?
6. Which data sources and chronology?
7. Which analysis answers the question?
8. Which threat is most dangerous?
9. What ethics, privacy, and access constraints apply?
10. What can a producer report and a consumer reasonably use?

## Quick distinctions

- Correlation predicts/associates; experiment addresses assigned causal effects.
- Survey describes populations; ethnography interprets culture-sharing life.
- Grounded theory explains process; narrative configures experience through stories.
- Mixed methods integrates conclusions; action research organizes accountable change cycles.

## Red flags

- No evidence deficiency behind the topic.
- Design label does not match actual procedures.
- Qualitative question contains predetermined variables/outcomes.
- Quantitative claim has no population or operational definitions.
- Integration appears only in the discussion.
- Action research reports success without cycles or contrary evidence.
- One validity procedure is treated as a guarantee.
- Recommendation exceeds design, context, or participant evidence.
