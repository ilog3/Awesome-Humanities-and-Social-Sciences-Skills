# Mixed Methods and Action Research

## Mixed integration

Select mixed methods only if integration answers a question neither component answers alone.

- **Convergent:** collect in parallel, analyze separately, merge and explain agreement/divergence.
- **Explanatory sequential:** quantitative results guide qualitative follow-up that explains selected findings.
- **Exploratory sequential:** qualitative findings inform instrument, variables, intervention, or quantitative phase.
- **Experimental/embedded:** one component is embedded to explain process, implementation, context, or experience around an experiment.

Specify priority, timing, sample relationship, connecting/building/merging/embedding procedure, joint display, integrated conclusion, and mixed-quality threats. Contradiction is a finding, not a defect to hide.

## Action cycles

Define a local problem with affected people, gather reconnaissance evidence, plan a bounded change, act, systematically observe, reflect/dialogue, and revise. Document cycles, theory of action, participation, burden, implementation, outcomes, unintended harms, and practitioner learning.

Practical action research aims at immediate improvement; participatory/critical forms additionally redistribute inquiry and action authority and address structural conditions. Do not call consultation participation.

## Ethics

Mixed and action designs may intensify linkage, identification, role conflict, coercion, and workload. Consent and data governance must cover cross-component integration and changing action plans.
