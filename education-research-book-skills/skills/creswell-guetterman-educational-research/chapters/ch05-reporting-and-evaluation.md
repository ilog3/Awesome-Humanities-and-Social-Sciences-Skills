# Reporting and Evaluation

## Report

Make the question-to-conclusion chain visible. Include title/abstract; problem and literature; purpose/questions/hypotheses; design; setting/participants/sample; instruments/protocols and researcher role; procedures/ethics; analysis; results/findings; interpretation; limits; implications; references; and appropriate appendices/materials.

Quantitative reports emphasize variables, sampling/assignment, measurement, estimates, uncertainty, diagnostics, and reproducibility. Qualitative reports emphasize context, relations, voice, evidence, interpretation, reflexivity, and transfer. Mixed reports must expose the interface and integrated inference. Action reports must show cycles, participation, change, and local accountability.

## Consumer evaluation

1. Identify problem, audience, and claim.
2. Confirm design features rather than trusting the label.
3. Test alignment of purpose, question, data, analysis, and conclusion.
4. Audit sample and scope.
5. Audit instrument/protocol and researcher influence.
6. Inspect missing data, contradictory evidence, deviations, and harms.
7. Examine validity/credibility and alternatives.
8. Separate statistical, practical, theoretical, and ethical significance.
9. Check whether recommendations follow and for whom.
10. Compare with the larger body of evidence.

Never evaluate quality by journal status, author reputation, complexity, or one checklist score alone.
