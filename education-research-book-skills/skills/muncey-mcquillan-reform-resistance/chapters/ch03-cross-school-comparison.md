# Cross-School Comparison

## Core idea

Comparing contrasting school histories reveals which conditions support reform and which apparent successes depend on local selection, leadership, resources, or scale.

## Comparison dimensions

- entry motive and initial coalition;
- student population and selection;
- reform scope and teacher participation;
- schedule, load, planning time, and budget;
- leadership continuity and distributed authority;
- classroom task and assessment change;
- student ownership and voice;
- relation to district and external accountability;
- response to turnover, conflict, and declining resources;
- depth, spread, ownership, and endurance.

## Worked example

Two schools both report smaller learning communities. In School A, teachers share planning time and follow students across years; in School B, the label changes but schedules and loads do not. Compare mechanism and experience, not program names. If A also selects motivated students, treat selection as a rival explanation for outcomes.

## Analytic rule

Use each case to challenge the explanation built from the others. Preserve configurations: a condition may support change only in combination with leadership, time, and participant ownership.

## Anti-patterns

- Ranking schools on one success score.
- Erasing case chronology in a variable table.
- Treating every difference as causal.
- Ignoring failed, partial, and reversed implementation.

## Key takeaways

1. Compare processes and configurations, not labels.
2. Separate reform reach from reform quality.
3. Longitudinal evidence distinguishes launch energy from endurance.
