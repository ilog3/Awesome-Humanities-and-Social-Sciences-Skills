# Local Agendas and School Trajectories

## Core idea

Schools join a reform for different reasons. Those agendas shape which principles receive attention, who owns the work, and how reform survives conflict and turnover.

## Frameworks introduced

- **Agenda map:** Compare reform purposes held by movement staff, district, principal, teachers, students, and families.
- **Trajectory analysis:** Follow recruitment, launch, early wins, conflict, staffing changes, resource shifts, expansion, decline, and institutionalization.
- **Translation chain:** Trace how a principle changes meaning from network discourse to school plan to classroom task to student experience.
- **Ownership distribution:** Examine who can interpret, decide, criticize, and revise the reform.

## Worked example

One school may join to rescue low-performing students, another to protect teacher autonomy, and another for status or grant resources. The same personalized program can therefore become a selective enclave, a whole-school redesign, or a temporary project. Build a year-by-year table linking agenda, actor coalition, organizational decision, classroom consequence, and student response.

## Resistance diagnosis

Before labeling resistance, identify whether actors reject the principle, its interpretation, the workload, exclusion from decisions, implementation timing, unequal risk, or contradiction with tests and schedules.

## Key takeaways

1. Membership does not create shared purpose.
2. Local adaptation must be documented as translation.
3. Personnel and political coalitions are causal parts of reform.
4. Resistance often reveals an unresolved design contradiction.
