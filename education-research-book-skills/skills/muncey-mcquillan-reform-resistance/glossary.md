# Glossary

- **Coalition of Essential Schools (CES)** — U.S. reform network organized around flexible common principles associated with Theodore Sizer.
- **Common principles** — Shared reform commitments intended for local interpretation rather than standardized implementation.
- **Congruence** — Alignment between pedagogy and organizational conditions such as time, load, budget, roles, and assessment.
- **Depth** — Degree to which practice and student experience embody the reform's learning principles.
- **Endurance** — Capacity of reform to persist through time, turnover, conflict, and resource change.
- **Exhibition of mastery** — Performance-based demonstration of consequential understanding.
- **Less is more** — Focus on fewer essential intellectual goals for deeper mastery.
- **Local translation** — Process by which actors interpret and reshape a reform principle in context.
- **Ownership** — Distribution of meaningful authority to interpret, decide, and revise reform.
- **Personalization** — Knowing learners well enough to adapt learning while sustaining common goals.
- **Political work** — Building authority, coalition, participation, protection, and resource alignment for change.
- **Resistance** — Situated opposition, noncompliance, reinterpretation, or withdrawal whose mechanism must be diagnosed.
- **Spread** — Distribution of reform across actors, classrooms, departments, and school structures.
- **Student-as-worker** — Principle that students undertake substantial intellectual production while teachers coach.
- **Teacher load** — Number and complexity of students/responsibilities; CES historically proposed a target below roughly 80 students per teacher.
- **Trajectory** — Reform's changing sequence from entry and launch through conflict, institutionalization, retreat, or renewal.
