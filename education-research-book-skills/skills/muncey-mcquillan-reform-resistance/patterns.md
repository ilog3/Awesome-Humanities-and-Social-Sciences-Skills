# Patterns

## Principle-to-practice trace

**When to use:** A school claims reform adoption.

**How:** For each principle trace network statement → local interpretation → organizational change → classroom task → student experience → evidence of learning.

**Trade-off:** Flexible principles enable adaptation but complicate fidelity judgments.

## Reform trajectory table

```text
Period | Actor coalition | Local agenda | Resources/structure | Classroom enactment | Student response | Resistance | Turning point
```

## Resistance diagnosis

**When to use:** Actors oppose, delay, or reinterpret change.

**How:** Identify contested object; collect actor rationale; test workload, coherence, skill, identity, authority, material, accountability, and exclusion mechanisms; observe consequence; redesign the relevant condition.

**Trade-off:** Some resistance protects inequity; some protects professional or student interests.

## Cross-school matrix

**When to use:** Comparing implementation.

**How:** Compare entry motive, ownership, leadership, time, load, selection, task change, student voice, accountability, depth, spread, and endurance. Retain a chronology for each case.

## Reform load audit

**When to use:** Change is repeatedly added but not sustained.

**How:** List new work, discontinued work, protected time, staffing, skill needs, coordination dependencies, and accountability conflicts. Require a subtraction or capacity decision for every major addition.
