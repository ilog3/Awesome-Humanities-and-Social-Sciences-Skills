# Reform Decision Cheatsheet

| Evidence | Diagnose next | Do not conclude yet |
|---|---|---|
| School joined a network | Local agenda and decision rights | Reform is implemented |
| Staff repeat principles | Tasks, schedules, assessment, student experience | Shared meaning exists |
| One classroom succeeds | Selection, resources, coordination, spread | Whole-school change |
| Teachers resist | Contested object, workload, coherence, voice, risk | Deficit or laziness |
| Students resist | Task clarity, sequencing, relevance, assessment | They reject deeper learning |
| Principal strongly supports | Distributed capacity and succession | Reform will endure |
| Professional development occurred | Practice change and follow-up support | Capability changed |
| Scores improved | Selection, baseline, pedagogy, other changes | Reform caused improvement |
| Reform expanded | Depth and resource dilution | Success scaled |

## Four outcome dimensions

- **Depth:** Did teaching and learning change meaningfully?
- **Spread:** Across how much of the school?
- **Ownership:** Who can interpret and revise it?
- **Endurance:** Does it survive turnover and constraint?

## Non-negotiable tests

- Educational theory plus political coalition.
- Pedagogy plus organizational alignment.
- Teacher agency plus student experience.
- Within-case history plus cross-case comparison.
- Additions plus explicit subtraction/capacity.
