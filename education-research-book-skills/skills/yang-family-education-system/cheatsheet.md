# Family-Education Systems Cheatsheet

## Fast diagnostic

Ask in order:

1. What capability, relationship, or safety issue is actually at stake?
2. What does the child experience and want?
3. What strengths already exist?
4. Which macro, meso, and micro conditions shape the issue?
5. Are family, school, and social messages aligned?
6. Does the goal balance participation with agency?
7. What is the smallest safe change, and who has power to make it?
8. What evidence and review date will guide revision?

## Decision table

| Situation | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Child framed as disobedient | Process and ecology map | Character judgment |
| Caregivers disagree | Core-value alignment plus flexible methods | Forced surface consistency |
| School blames family | Responsibility and resource map | Outsourcing school duties |
| Family category treated as risk | Strengths-and-needs protocol | Deficit stereotype |
| Practice produces compliance but distress | Hidden-harm audit | Equating obedience with learning |
| Adults seek a universal technique | Goal–method fit and small experiment | Recipe thinking |
| Rapid social or digital change | Learning-family cycle | Total insulation or surveillance |
| Complex safety, legal, or clinical issue | Safeguard and refer | Acting beyond competence |

## Minimum useful output

- Issue and evidence
- Child voice
- Strengths and needs
- Ecology map
- Value and goal
- Socialization–individualization balance
- Family, school, and community responsibilities
- Method plus harm audit
- Indicators, review date, and referral threshold

## Historical caution

Treat demographic claims, policy references, psychological generalizations, and labels in the 2003 book as historically situated. Verify current facts and use contemporary child-rights, disability, safeguarding, and family-diversity standards.
