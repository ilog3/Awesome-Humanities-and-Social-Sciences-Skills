# Large Education and Family-Education Studies

## Core idea

Family education becomes intelligible when placed inside social transformation and a complete education system. Its study requires theory, evidence, and interdisciplinary methods rather than intuition or commercial “secrets.”

## Frameworks introduced

- **Large-education view:** Connect family, school, and society horizontally and trace historical change vertically.
- **Science–art distinction:** Research seeks regularities and evidence; practice requires situated judgment. They complement rather than exclude each other.
- **Instinct-to-reflection development:** Caregiving may draw on affection and experience, but deliberate educational action can be examined, learned, and improved.
- **Disciplinary construction:** Define object, nature, tasks, theoretical foundations, methods, and conceptual system.

## Worked example

A child uses short-video platforms late at night. A narrow technique asks which parental control works. The large-education analysis includes household routines, parental work hours, device design, peer norms, school workload, digital literacy, platform policy, and the child's purposes. The intervention allocates responsibilities across levels rather than blaming one caregiver.

## Anti-patterns

- Universal parenting recipe.
- Marketed “secret” based on one family.
- Treating family as closed and socially isolated.
- Rejecting theory because practice needs judgment.

## Key takeaways

1. Connect family practice to wider education and society.
2. Separate evidence from persuasive anecdote.
3. Use multiple disciplines but maintain a clear educational question.
