# Values, Goals, and Functions

## Core idea

Family-education goals must be derived from a defensible view of human development and converted into capabilities, not left as slogans or reduced to school achievement.

## Frameworks introduced

- **Value–goal chain:** Values justify purposes; goals specify developmental direction; practices create conditions; evaluation checks effects.
- **Socialization–individualization balance:** Support participation in social life and development of agency and uniqueness.
- **Individual functions:** Socialization, individualization, competence, identity, and well-being.
- **Social functions:** Cultural transmission, civic/political participation, economic capability, and social continuity/change.
- **Function combination:** One practice can have intended, unintended, positive, negative, immediate, and delayed effects.

## Worked example

If the stated goal is “responsibility,” define child-appropriate capabilities: planning a task, considering others, asking for help, repairing harm, and reflecting on consequences. Choose household participation and dialogue, not fear-based compliance. Check whether the practice builds responsibility across settings or merely obedience in front of adults.

## Anti-patterns

- Goal equals exam score.
- Adult convenience presented as child development.
- Declared intent treated as demonstrated function.
- Socialization used to suppress difference.

## Key takeaways

1. Make values explicit and goals observable.
2. Evaluate unintended functions.
3. Protect both belonging and agency.
