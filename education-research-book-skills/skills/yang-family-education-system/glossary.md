# Glossary

- **Family education:** Intentional and unintentional learning occurring through household relationships, care, communication, routines, example, and joint life.
- **Large education:** A perspective that treats education as a society-wide, lifelong ecology extending beyond formal schooling.
- **Educational ecology:** Interdependence among family, school, community, culture, institutions, resources, and historical conditions.
- **Socialization:** Learning the meanings, norms, roles, skills, and relations needed for social participation.
- **Individualization:** Development of agency, judgment, interests, creativity, and a distinctive self.
- **Value orientation:** The account of what and whose good education should serve.
- **Educational goal:** A contextually specified capability, disposition, relationship, or condition that guides action and assessment.
- **Function:** A realized or potential consequence of family education for individuals, families, or society; not identical to intention.
- **Macro influence:** Broad policy, economic, cultural, technological, or demographic condition.
- **Meso influence:** Institutional and network condition linking households to schools, work, community, and services.
- **Micro influence:** Immediate household resource, relationship, belief, routine, or interaction.
- **Family-education guidance:** Collaborative support that helps families understand situations, select actions, and evaluate consequences.
- **Parent education:** Adult learning related to caregiving and family educational responsibility; it should not be reduced to correction of parental deficits.
- **Protective ecology:** Relationships, resources, critical capacities, and institutions that support safety, interpretation, resilience, and help-seeking.
- **Learning family:** A household that makes inquiry, reflection, co-learning, adaptation, and shared responsibility part of everyday life.
- **Child voice:** Meaningful, age-appropriate opportunity for children to express experience and influence matters affecting them.
- **Deficit labeling:** Inferring incapacity or risk from a person's or family's category rather than examining evidence, strengths, needs, and structures.
