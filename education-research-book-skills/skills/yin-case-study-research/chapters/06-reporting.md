# 报告案例研究

## Design for an audience

Identify whether the audience is academic, policy, professional, participant, public, or mixed. Their background and decisions affect length, terminology, evidence presentation, and medium, but not the obligation to report method and uncertainty honestly.

## Compositional structures

Possible structures include linear-analytic, comparative, chronological, theory-building, suspense, and unsequenced modular forms. Multiple-case reports may present full cases followed by synthesis, questions/themes across cases, or a mixed form.

## Writing process

Begin early. A provisional outline reveals missing evidence and can organize the database. Draft sections with the strongest evidence first if useful. Integrate tables, timelines, diagrams, and quantitative data where they sharpen the case logic.

## Evidence presentation

Provide enough evidence for readers to judge the analysis, including contrary material and source conditions. Preserve the chain of evidence without dumping the database into the report. Key participants may review factual and interpretive drafts, but their approval is not a substitute for analysis.

## Anonymity and review

Prefer disclosure of case identity when ethically and legally appropriate because it supports verification and context. Use anonymity when harm, privacy, legal duties, or access conditions require it; explain the analytic loss and prevent deductive identification.

Simulate skeptical review: method fit, case boundary, data adequacy, rival explanations, analytic generalization, ethics, and evidentiary traceability. A good case report is significant, complete within its boundary, attentive to alternatives, evidence-rich, and engagingly composed.
