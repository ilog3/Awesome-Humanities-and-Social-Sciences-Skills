# 案例研究作为研究方法

## Definition

A case study is an empirical inquiry that investigates a contemporary phenomenon in depth and within its real-world context, especially when the boundary between phenomenon and context is not evident. It responds to this situation by using multiple sources of evidence whose data converge and by benefiting from prior theoretical propositions that guide collection and analysis.

## Three method conditions

Choose among research strategies by asking:

1. Is the question “who/what/where/how many,” “how,” or “why”?
2. Does the researcher require control over behavioral events?
3. Is the focus contemporary or historical?

Case studies are strong for contemporary how/why questions without experimental control. They can be exploratory, descriptive, or explanatory and can include qualitative and quantitative data.

## Case versus adjacent terms

- **Teaching case:** designed for learning and discussion, not necessarily a complete research inquiry.
- **Case report/anecdote:** describes an instance without a full design and evidence logic.
- **Qualitative research:** broader family; a case study may also use quantitative evidence.
- **History:** shares contextual explanation but usually lacks direct observation or interviews with contemporary actors.

## Bound the case

Name the entity or process and set temporal, spatial, organizational, and participant boundaries. If the proposed case cannot be distinguished from its context at all, narrow or redefine it. If several possible cases exist, specify why one unit is primary.
