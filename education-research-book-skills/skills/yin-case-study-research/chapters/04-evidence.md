# 收集案例研究证据

## Six sources

- **Documentation:** stable and reviewable but may be biased, incomplete, or inaccessible.
- **Archival records:** precise and sometimes quantitative, but created for another purpose and subject to access limits.
- **Interviews:** targeted and explanatory, yet influenced by memory, wording, reflexivity, and social desirability.
- **Direct observation:** contextual and real-time, but selective, time-intensive, and reactive.
- **Participant observation:** provides insider access and agency while increasing bias, obligation, and role conflict.
- **Physical artifacts:** reveal material, technical, or cultural features but require interpretive context.

Match sources to propositions. No source is intrinsically superior.

## Three collection principles

1. **Use multiple sources** and seek converging lines on the same fact or finding.
2. **Create a case-study database** separate from the final narrative: notes, documents, tabular data, artifacts, metadata, and investigator answers.
3. **Maintain a chain of evidence** so a reader can move from question to protocol, source, database item, finding, and conclusion—and back again.

## Interview stance

Operate on two levels: follow the formal line of inquiry and ask conversational, non-threatening questions. Treat interviewees as knowledgeable participants, not passive data containers. Seek concrete events and corroborate factual claims.

## Evidence governance

Record source, date, creator, collection circumstances, permissions, version, confidentiality, and reliability concerns. Preserve raw evidence while keeping analysis copies. Minimize sensitive data and prevent a public report from becoming the only archive.
