# 开展研究前的准备

## Investigator skills

A case researcher should ask good questions, listen without being trapped by preconceptions, adapt to unexpected events, understand the issues under study, and remain unbiased enough to recognize contrary evidence. Flexibility is disciplined redesign, not ad hoc abandonment of the protocol.

## Protocol

Prepare:

1. overview, purpose, theory, sponsors, and audience;
2. field procedures, access, schedule, credentials, ethics, and contingencies;
3. case-study questions for the investigator—not a respondent questionnaire—organized by evidence need;
4. provisional report outline and data formats.

Use multiple levels of questions: individual evidence prompts, case-level questions, pattern questions, cross-case questions, and implications beyond the case.

## Team training

Train all investigators on case boundaries, propositions, terminology, evidence handling, interview stance, ethics, and unexpected scenarios. Practice mock encounters and compare interpretations. Resolve design flaws before deployment.

## Pilot case

A pilot refines content, procedures, logistics, questions, access, and analysis—not merely the instrument. Choose it for convenience, accessibility, or methodological learning rather than as a formal replication. Record what changed and why.

## Readiness gate

Do not begin full collection until the team can explain: the case, unit, propositions, rivals, source plan, protocol, database, consent and security plan, escalation route, and preliminary analysis logic.
