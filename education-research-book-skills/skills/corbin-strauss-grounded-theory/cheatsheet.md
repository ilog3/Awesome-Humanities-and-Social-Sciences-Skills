# Grounded Theory Cheatsheet

## Each analysis cycle

1. Select a manageable segment.
2. Ask what action, problem, condition, and consequence appear.
3. Compare with another incident.
4. Name or revise a concept.
5. Develop properties and dimensions.
6. Write a dated memo with source links.
7. Diagram plausible relations.
8. Identify a negative case or gap.
9. Choose the next theoretical sample.
10. Return with new data and compare again.

## Before claiming a category

- It is more than a topic label.
- Multiple incidents support it.
- Similarities and differences are explicit.
- Properties and dimensional variation are developed.
- Conditions, actions, and consequences are tested.
- Negative evidence is recorded.
- Its relation to other categories is memoed.

## Before claiming saturation

- Name the exact category or relation.
- Show the sampling steps taken to develop it.
- Demonstrate variation and negative cases.
- Explain what recent data added.
- State remaining access or resource limits.

## Before claiming theory

- A central storyline integrates major categories.
- Context and process are explanatory, not decorative.
- Relations are grounded and bounded.
- The model handles variation and contrary cases.
- Memos preserve the data-to-theory chain.

## Red flags

- Coding everything before analyzing anything.
- Treating software trees as theory.
- Renaming interview topics as categories.
- Predetermining theoretical samples.
- Counting frequency as explanatory importance.
- Declaring saturation because interviews feel repetitive.
- Forcing data into the coding paradigm.
- Reporting a diagram without an audit trail.
