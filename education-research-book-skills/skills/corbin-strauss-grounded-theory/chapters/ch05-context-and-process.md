# Context and Process

## Context as layered conditions

Analyze conditions at personal, interactional, organizational, community, political, economic, historical, and global levels. Do not list context as background. Explain which conditions enter the action situation, how actors interpret them, and how they enable or constrain responses.

Use a conditional matrix or layered map when it helps move between micro and macro levels. Avoid claiming every distant condition directly causes the focal action.

## Action, interaction, and emotion

Study strategic and routine action, interaction among actors, nonaction, and emotion as part of response. Identify contingencies, turning points, negotiations, and unintended consequences.

## Process

Process is patterned action/interaction unfolding in response to changing conditions. Trace phases, sequence, duration, tempo, transitions, feedback, breakdown, recovery, and variation. A chronology alone is not a process analysis; specify the mechanism that links one phase to the next.

Build a process table:

```text
Phase | Conditions | Actor interpretation | Action/interaction | Consequence | Transition mechanism | Variation
```

Re-read earlier memos and original data to test whether the proposed process appears across cases, only under specific conditions, or not at all.
