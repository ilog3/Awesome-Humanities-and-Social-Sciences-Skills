# Analysis and Concept Formation

## Microanalysis

Read a small segment closely to expose assumptions, actions, conditions, meanings, and consequences. Use it most intensively early on, for puzzling passages, or when a category needs development; do not line-by-line code everything forever.

Ask generative questions: What is happening? What problem is being managed? What action is taken? Under what conditions? What changes? What are the consequences? What alternative interpretation fits?

## Constant comparison

Compare incidents for similarities and differences. Give similar incidents a provisional conceptual name, then use variation to identify properties and dimensions. Compare new incidents with the concept and split, rename, merge, or discard it when needed.

## From label to category

A topical code such as “support” merely locates content. An analytic concept such as “borrowing legitimacy” names an action or mechanism. Develop it by specifying who borrows legitimacy, from whom, under what risk, through which acts, and with what outcomes.

A category is a higher-level concept supported by related concepts and substantial variation. Define:

- properties—the characteristics that constitute it;
- dimensions—the ranges along which properties vary;
- conditions—what enables, constrains, or shapes it;
- action/interaction—what actors do or feel in response;
- consequences—intended and unintended outcomes.

Use the condition–action/interaction–consequence frame as a heuristic, not a cage.

## Worked example

Incident: a novice teacher asks a senior colleague to publicly endorse a contested grading decision.

- Topic label: colleague support.
- Analytic concept: borrowing legitimacy.
- Property: visibility of endorsement, from private advice to public defense.
- Condition: perceived challenge to novice authority.
- Action: recruit a higher-status ally.
- Consequence: short-term stabilization, possible long-term dependency.
- Next comparison: cases where novices refuse help, obtain peer rather than senior endorsement, or reverse the decision.
