# Theoretical Sampling

Theoretical sampling is data collection directed by concepts emerging from analysis. It is not a demographic sampling plan fixed in advance.

## Cycle

1. Analyze current data.
2. Identify an underdeveloped category, property, relation, or rival explanation.
3. Formulate a targeted analytic question.
4. Select people, events, sites, documents, or comparisons likely to answer it.
5. Collect only enough focused data to advance the question.
6. Compare, revise the category, and memo the decision.
7. Repeat until relevant categories are dense and integrated.

Example: if “borrowing legitimacy” appears only under public challenge, seek private challenges, high- and low-status allies, failed borrowing, and actors who maintain legitimacy alone.

## Saturation

Saturation is category-specific. Look for stable definition, documented variation, developed properties and dimensions, understood conditions and consequences, tested relations, and diminishing analytic returns. Report residual gaps. Time, access, and resources may require ending before saturation; call this a limitation, not saturation.

In teams, circulate current memos and diagrams so collectors understand the conceptual purpose of new sampling.
