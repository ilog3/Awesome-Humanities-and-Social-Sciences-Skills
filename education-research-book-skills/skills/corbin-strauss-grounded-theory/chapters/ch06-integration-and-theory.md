# Integration and Theory

## From categories to an explanatory whole

Integration begins throughout analysis but becomes explicit when major categories are sufficiently developed. Re-read and sort memos, tell the analytic story in plain language, draw competing diagrams, and identify the concept that best organizes the variation.

A central category should be analytically powerful, recurrent, related to major categories, capable of explaining variation, and supported by data. Do not select it merely because it is frequent.

## Integration procedure

1. Write a one-page storyline without quotations.
2. List major categories and each one's contribution.
3. Map conditions → actions/interactions → consequences.
4. Insert process and contextual variation.
5. Test the scheme against cases and negative cases.
6. Locate thin categories and sample or reanalyze.
7. Compare the emergent theory with relevant literature.
8. Write bounded propositions specifying when relations hold.

Distinguish a theme inventory from theory. Theory links concepts into an explanatory account of how and why action varies under conditions.

## Density and parsimony

Aim for enough categories to explain important variation, but not so many that the account becomes a catalog. Subordinate, merge, or remove concepts that do not advance the central story while retaining audit links to the data.
