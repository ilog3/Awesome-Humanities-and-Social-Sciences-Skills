# Qualitative Research Cheatsheet

## Before fieldwork

- Define phenomenon, boundaries, and claim type.
- State paradigm and researcher purposes.
- Draw the interactive design.
- Name purposive-sampling logic and limits.
- Map positionality, gatekeepers, participant consent, privacy, risk, and reciprocity.
- Prepare a flexible guide and data-management plan.

## During fieldwork

- Renew consent when conditions change.
- Ask concrete open questions; probe example, process, meaning, and exception.
- Listen through silence; avoid evaluation and argument.
- Observe openly, then focus and respond.
- Separate event, speech, inference, emotion, method, theory, and ethics.
- Analyze immediately and let evidence redirect sampling.

## During analysis

- Preserve source links and cases before fragmenting.
- Find local concepts and define codes.
- Combine categorical comparison with contextual reconstruction.
- Search for negative evidence and rival explanations.
- Memo the path from material to concept and relation.
- Claim no more theory or transfer than evidence supports.

## Before reporting

- Audit descriptive, interpretive, theoretical, and evaluative claims.
- Show context, selection, relationship, reactivity, and uncertainty.
- Protect against deductive identification.
- Distinguish participant voice from authorial construction.
- Explain who may use the result, where, and under what conditions.

## Red flags

| Red flag | Repair |
|---|---|
| “Qualitative means no hypothesis or design.” | Use a flexible interactive design and disclose revisions. |
| “We interviewed whoever was available.” | Name convenience limits or redesign purposively. |
| “Themes emerged.” | Show coding, comparison, context, and interpretive decisions. |
| “Participants confirmed validity.” | Treat feedback as one situated evidence source. |
| “Three sources agree, so it is true.” | Test shared bias, power, and contradictory evidence. |
| “The findings generalize.” | Specify internal scope and the basis for transfer. |
| “Ethics approval solved ethics.” | Reassess consent, risk, power, privacy, and reciprocity throughout. |
| “Mixed methods are automatically stronger.” | State linked questions and the integration logic. |

## Historical note

The book dates from 2000. Its framework remains useful, but current ethics review, data security, recording technology, digital-fieldwork practice, and disciplinary standards must be checked independently.
