# Action Research and Method Combination

## Action research

Use action research when practitioners or affected participants investigate practice while trying to improve it. Define whose problem, whose action, whose knowledge, and whose authority are involved.

Use a revisable cycle:

`identify and understand → plan → act → observe/document → reflect/dialogue → reframe and plan again`

Distinguish reflection in action, reflection on action, and critical examination of the values and structures organizing action. Rigor depends on an evidence trail, alternative explanations, dialogue, transparent adaptation, and documentation of both improvement and harm—not on success stories alone.

Participation must be meaningful. Avoid calling a project participatory when participants only supply data or implement researchers' decisions.

## Qualitative–quantitative combination

Combine approaches only when linked questions require different forms of evidence and the assumptions are compatible enough for the intended claim.

### Whole-study combination

Keep qualitative and quantitative components relatively complete, using a sequential, concurrent/main-plus-supporting, or deliberately mixed design. Specify priority, timing, points of connection, and who interprets disagreement.

### Component-level combination

Combine selected techniques or forms of evidence within stages—for example, a structured distribution to guide purposive case selection or contextual interviews to interpret a survey pattern. Do not assume a technique is philosophically neutral; state how it is being used.

## Integration table

For each component record:

- question and claim type;
- methodological assumptions;
- sample and unit;
- data form and timing;
- analysis;
- integration point;
- agreement, complementarity, or contradiction;
- inference boundary;
- ethics and participant burden.

Do not use one component merely to decorate or validate the other. A contradiction can be an analytic result that exposes different levels, contexts, measures, or meanings.
