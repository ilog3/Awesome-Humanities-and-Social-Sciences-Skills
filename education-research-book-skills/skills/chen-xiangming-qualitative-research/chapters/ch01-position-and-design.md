# Position and Interactive Design

## Methodological position

Before procedures, state:

- **ontology:** what kind of phenomenon is assumed to exist;
- **epistemology:** how researcher and participants can know it;
- **methodology:** the logic linking questions, relations, materials, and claims;
- **method/technique:** the concrete process and tools.

Qualitative work commonly assumes meanings are contextual, knowledge is co-produced, the researcher is an instrument of inquiry, and findings are interpretations open to dialogue and revision. Critical approaches additionally examine domination and transformation. Do not pretend all qualitative traditions share one philosophy.

## Interactive design

Hold these components in reciprocal alignment:

1. phenomenon and boundary;
2. research questions;
3. personal, practical, and scientific purposes;
4. literature, experiential knowledge, and conceptual framework;
5. sampling and relationships;
6. data generation;
7. analysis and theory building;
8. validity, inference, ethics, and representation.

Changing a question may require a new sample and collection method; a field relationship may reveal a new ethics risk; early analysis may revise the conceptual frame. Record each consequential change and its rationale.

## Question design

Prefer questions about meaning, experience, process, interaction, context, mechanisms, and difference. Classify the intended claim:

- descriptive: what is happening?
- interpretive: what does it mean to participants?
- theoretical: what concepts or relations explain it?
- transfer/inference: where else might the account matter?
- evaluative: by what values is it judged?

Keep evaluative and transfer claims especially explicit; they require additional warrants.

## Conceptual framework

Build a provisional map from literature, prior research, theory, and the researcher's experience. Use it to focus attention and expose assumptions, not to force data into preselected boxes. Include rival concepts and revise the map throughout fieldwork.

## Proposal versus design

A proposal is the communicated plan at a point in time. The design is the living logic of the study. Preserve approved ethical boundaries while documenting justified adaptation.
