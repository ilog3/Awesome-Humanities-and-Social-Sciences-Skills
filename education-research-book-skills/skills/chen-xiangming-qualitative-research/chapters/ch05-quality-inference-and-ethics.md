# Quality, Inference, and Ethics

## Paradigm-aware quality

Quality criteria follow the inquiry's assumptions and purpose, but this does not mean “anything goes.” State the criteria, evidence, and audience. Dialogue across paradigms requires understanding the other's premises before critique.

## Validity targets

- **Descriptive validity:** accuracy of observable events, words, dates, and settings.
- **Interpretive validity:** adequacy of the account of participants' meanings.
- **Theoretical validity:** fit and explanatory power of concepts and relations.
- **Evaluative validity:** defensibility and transparency of value judgments.

Investigate memory error, research reactivity, cultural presupposition, indirect sources, selective attention, status/power effects, premature closure, and confirmation bias.

Useful tests include prolonged and varied engagement, searching for disconfirming cases, source and method comparison, participant feedback, peer challenge, rich original material, comparison, and repeated movement between parts and whole. Each has limits: participants can disagree with an interpretation for substantive reasons, and multiple sources can share the same bias.

## Inference and transfer

Distinguish:

- **internal inference:** extending evidence within the studied case, people, time, or setting;
- **external transfer:** relevance beyond the sample through reader recognition, theoretical explanation, or reasoned comparison—not statistical representativeness.

Enable transfer by specifying context, selection logic, process, variation, mechanism, limits, and evidence. Let readers assess similarity while stating what the study can and cannot support.

## Ethics throughout the relationship

Use current law and institutional rules in addition to these principles:

- voluntary, continuing, informed participation and truthful disclosure;
- privacy, confidentiality, data minimization, and secure retention;
- fairness in selection, interaction, interpretation, and conflict;
- proportionate reciprocity without undue inducement;
- respectful exit, feedback, and handling of later requests;
- special safeguards for vulnerability, dependency, sensitive topics, and foreseeable harm.

Ethics is not completed by a consent form. Reassess risk when roles, questions, audiences, data linkage, technology, or publication plans change. Gatekeepers cannot waive individual consent. Explain the limits of confidentiality and any mandatory reporting before sensitive disclosure where feasible.

## Claim audit

For every major conclusion, attach evidence, context, interpretive steps, contrary material, researcher's role, participant perspectives, uncertainty, transfer boundary, ethical cost, and what new evidence would change the claim.
