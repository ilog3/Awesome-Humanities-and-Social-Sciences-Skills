# Research Methods Cheatsheet

## Ten checks

1. Is the problem empirical and bounded?
2. Are variables/phenomena and terms operationally clear?
3. Does literature justify the question and design?
4. Which design creates the needed comparison or interpretation?
5. Is variation controlled without destroying context?
6. Are selection and assignment distinguished?
7. Does measurement support the intended interpretation?
8. Does analysis match design, scale, and assumptions?
9. What are the strongest rival explanation and scope limit?
10. Does the report reveal enough to evaluate the conclusion?

## Design quick map

| Need | Design |
|---|---|
| Randomized causal contrast | True experiment |
| Intervention without random assignment | Quasi-experiment |
| Pre-existing relation/group | Nonexperimental quantitative |
| Contextualized meaning/process | Qualitative |
| Past development | Historical |
| Culture in natural setting | Ethnographic |
| Linked evidence forms | Mixed |
| Structured representation | Modeling |
| Iterative expert judgment | Delphi |

## Red flags

- “Random” without saying selection or assignment.
- One-group pre/post causal claim.
- Different teachers, tests, or schools confounded with treatments.
- Correlation described as impact.
- Response rate used as the only survey-quality evidence.
- Instrument name used instead of a validity argument.
- Software output substituted for analysis.
- Delphi agreement reported without panel and attrition.
- Recommendations broader than sample, design, or evidence.
