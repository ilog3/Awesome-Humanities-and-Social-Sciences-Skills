# Sampling, Measurement, and Statistics

## Sampling

For population inference define target population, accessible population, frame, element, selection probability, sample size rationale, and nonresponse. Use simple random, stratified, cluster, or systematic sampling according to structure; inspect unequal probabilities, intracluster similarity, periodicity, coverage, and design effects.

For sampling choices from assignment. In qualitative designs name the information purpose of comprehensive, maximum-variation, extreme, typical, homogeneous, or other purposive selection and the stopping logic.

## Measurement and data collection

Define construct and intended interpretation/use, choose or build tests, questionnaires, rating scales, observation systems, and records, and plan scoring/coding. Evaluate reliability/precision and a validity argument based on content, response processes, internal structure, relations, fairness, and consequences. Standardize administration where required and examine missing or inaccurate response.

## Descriptive statistics

Inspect distributions before summarizing. Report counts, missingness, center, spread, shape, standardized scores when meaningful, and correlations with plots. Match summaries to scale and skew; correlation does not establish causation.

## Inferential statistics

Define population parameter/estimand, sampling or assignment basis, null and alternative, error rates, interval estimate, assumptions, and analysis plan. The book introduces t procedures, analysis of variance, chi-square, correlation, analysis of covariance, and meta-analysis. Use current implementations and diagnostics; account for clustering, repeated measures, multiplicity, missing data, and model dependence as relevant.

Report estimates, uncertainty intervals, effect sizes, exact procedures, assumption checks, sensitivity, and practical meaning—not only statistical significance.

## Ethics

Use current review requirements, voluntary consent/assent, safeguarding, equitable recruitment, confidentiality limits, secure data, minimization, and responsible reporting. Historical guidance in the book does not replace present law or institutional policy.
