# Mixed Methods, Modeling, and Delphi

## Mixed methods

Define linked questions and why one form of evidence is insufficient. Specify priority, sequence, samples, data, analyses, integration point, and inference. Integration can occur through sampling, building, merging, embedding, joint display, or interpretation. A quasi-experiment plus survey is useful only if the survey explains, contextualizes, or tests a defined part of the intervention logic.

## Modeling

Use a model to represent relevant elements and relations, not to decorate a theory. Define purpose and boundary; identify components and assumptions; formalize relations; estimate or calibrate where appropriate; check internal coherence; compare predictions/representations with evidence; perform sensitivity analysis; revise; and state where the model fails. Do not confuse fit with truth or omit uncertainty and alternative structures.

## Delphi

Use structured, iterative expert judgment when evidence is dispersed, forecasting or prioritization is needed, and direct group interaction may create dominance. Define expertise and panel diversity; recruit transparently; run anonymous rounds; synthesize responses; return controlled feedback; permit revision; track attrition and stability; predefine stopping; and report the distribution and reasons for disagreement.

Consensus can reflect panel construction, question framing, fatigue, conformity to feedback, or shared blind spots. It is a documented judgment, not empirical proof.

## Selection rule

Choose these approaches when their additional structure changes the warranted conclusion. State their extra burden, participant risk, and failure modes.
