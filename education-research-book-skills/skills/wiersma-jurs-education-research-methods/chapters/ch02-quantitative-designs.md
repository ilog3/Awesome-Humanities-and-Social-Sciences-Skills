# Quantitative Designs

## Variation-control principle

A good design makes the focal systematic variation identifiable, controls plausible extraneous variation, and reduces random/error variation without creating artificial conditions that destroy relevance.

## True experiments

Define treatment, comparator, unit of random assignment, outcome, timing, and estimand. Common structures include posttest-only randomized control, pretest–posttest randomized control, Solomon four-group, factorial, and repeated-measures designs. Address internal, external, construct, and statistical-conclusion validity; clustering, fidelity, attrition, contamination, testing, and multiple outcomes.

## Quasi-experiments

Use when random assignment is infeasible but a credible counterfactual can be constructed. The book covers posttest-only and pretest–posttest nonequivalent groups, interrupted time series and variants, and single-subject structures such as A–B and A–B–A. Diagnose selection, differential trends, history, maturation, regression, instrumentation, anticipation, seasonality, autocorrelation, carryover, and irreversibility. Modern stronger variants should be considered where appropriate.

## Nonexperimental quantitative research

- **Survey:** describe or relate characteristics using a defensible frame, sample, instrument, response process, and error analysis.
- **Correlational:** estimate form and magnitude of relations; inspect confounding, range restriction, nonlinearity, measurement error, clustering, and temporal order.
- **Ex post facto/causal-comparative:** compare pre-existing groups to explore plausible explanations; call causal claims tentative because exposure was not assigned.
- **Interview survey:** standardize administration sufficiently for the intended comparison while monitoring interviewer and social-desirability effects.

## Interpretation

Random assignment supports causal comparability under conditions; random selection supports population inference under conditions. One does not substitute for the other. Report effect size, uncertainty, assumptions, heterogeneity, practical importance, and limitations.
