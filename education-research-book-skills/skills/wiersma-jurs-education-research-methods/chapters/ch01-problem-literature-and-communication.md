# Problem, Literature, and Communication

## Problem specification

Start from an educational concern but state a researchable relation or phenomenon. Define constants and variables, independent/dependent roles only when the design warrants them, operational definitions, population/case, context, and boundaries. A hypothesis predicts a relation; a null hypothesis is a statistical testing device, not the researcher's belief.

Test significance, researchability, feasibility, ethics, empirical access, theoretical connection, and contribution. Keep the problem statement free from conclusions already assumed.

## Literature work

Search appropriate databases and reference trails with recorded terms and limits. Select sources by relevance, quality, recency/historical importance, and method. Extract question, theory, sample, design, measurement, analysis, result, and limitation. Organize by issue and method, critically compare, identify unresolved problems, and show consequences for the design. Update the book's database and citation instructions to current systems.

## Proposal

Include problem, rationale/significance, literature, questions/hypotheses, method, sample, variables/phenomena, instruments/protocols, procedures, analysis, validity/reliability, ethics, schedule, resources, and dissemination. A proposal promises a logic, not predetermined positive results.

## Report

Keep methods reproducible or auditable and results separate from interpretation. Report deviations, missingness, nulls, adverse events, contradictions, uncertainty, and limitations. Match abstract, tables, figures, references, appendices, presentation, and defense to the audience without changing the evidence.

## Critical appraisal

Read in two passes: first reconstruct the study's intended logic; then test every link. Ask whether the conclusion answers the question, the design supports the inference, the sample supports the scope, evidence supports operationalization, analysis fits the data, and recommendations introduce unstated values or assumptions.
