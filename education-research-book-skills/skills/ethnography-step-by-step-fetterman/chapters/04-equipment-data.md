# 民族志装备与资料治理

## Start with low-friction tools

Pen and paper remain useful because they are flexible, quiet, and rapidly deployable. Audio improves recall of wording and interaction; photography maps material and spatial patterns; video supports repeated analysis of sequence and gesture; film and edited media create public representations as well as records.

## Tool selection test

For each device ask:

1. What evidentiary problem does it solve?
2. How will it change participants' behavior and access?
3. What consent, privacy, ownership, and security risks arise?
4. Who can operate and interpret it competently?
5. How will records link to fieldnotes and analysis?

## Computers and networks

Digital tools can store, search, code, compare, map, share, and retrieve field material. Online resources and virtual offices aid collaboration but expand the field of exposure. Establish file naming, metadata, access control, encryption, backup, versioning, and retention/deletion rules before collection scales.

## Database design

Keep stable identifiers for events, people, places, documents, codes, consent restrictions, and source files. Link excerpts back to context. A database that fragments material without preserving episodes undermines ethnographic interpretation.

## Caution

Recording capacity creates selection and surveillance power. Collect the minimum needed, provide camera-free or recording-free options, and never infer that publicly accessible online content is ethically unrestricted research data.
