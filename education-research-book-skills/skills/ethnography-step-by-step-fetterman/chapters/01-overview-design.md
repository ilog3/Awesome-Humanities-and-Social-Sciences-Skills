# 概览：问题、理论与设计

Ethnography is the art and science of describing a group or culture. Like careful investigative reporting, it speaks with people, examines records, weighs conflicting accounts, traces interests and organization, and communicates a warranted story. Its distinctive focus is patterned, ordinary life rather than novelty alone.

## Orient the study

- **Problem:** What social world, practice, contradiction, or experience needs understanding?
- **Basic or applied:** Is the primary aim conceptual understanding, practical decision support, evaluation, or a combination?
- **Theory:** What sensitizing ideas guide attention, and what observations could revise them?
- **Design:** Where, with whom, for how long, through what roles and evidence?
- **Analysis:** How will claims be challenged during fieldwork rather than after it?
- **Product:** Who needs what form of account, and what consequences may follow?

## Flexible design

Create a fieldwork plan with boundaries, milestones, resources, and ethical review, but expect it to change as cultural knowledge develops. Preserve a chain from question to evidence and interpretation while allowing new questions to emerge.

## Quality

Readers should be able to see how the ethnographer came to a claim, what evidence supports it, which alternatives were considered, and how close collaborators, broader group members, documents, and observed behavior align or differ.
