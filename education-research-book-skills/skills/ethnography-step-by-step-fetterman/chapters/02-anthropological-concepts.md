# 人类学指引性概念

## Culture and holism

Culture is learned, shared, contested, and enacted knowledge that helps people interpret and organize life. Do not reify it as a fixed container. Use a holistic perspective to trace relevant relations among belief, language, work, status, material environment, history, and institutions.

## Contextualization

An action gains meaning from situation, relationship, sequence, place, and history. Record what happened before and after, who was present, and what participants considered at stake.

## Emic and etic

- **Emic:** categories and realities meaningful to participants.
- **Etic:** analytical and comparative concepts used by the researcher.

Move back and forth. Emic understanding prevents premature imposition; etic interpretation supports comparison and explanation. Neither is automatically neutral or complete.

## Multiple realities and diversity

Expect cultural variation within a group by role, generation, status, gender, location, biography, and situation. Contradictory accounts may reveal structure rather than error.

## Structure, function, symbol, ritual

Map positions and patterned relationships, then ask how practices operate and for whom. Symbols condense layered meanings; rituals organize sequence, participation, identity, transition, and power. Avoid assuming that persistence proves beneficial function.

## Micro, macro, operationalism

Link face-to-face events to organizations, policy, economy, and history. Translate broad concepts into observable indicators without reducing them to a single measure. Let local usage correct the operational definition.
