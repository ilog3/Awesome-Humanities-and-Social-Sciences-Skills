# 田野方法与技术

## Selection and sampling

Select cases purposively for relevance, access, contrast, diversity, typicality, key position, and emerging theory. Sample across people, spaces, times, events, and social roles. Document opportunity and gatekeeper effects.

## Entrée and relationships

Identify formal and informal gatekeepers. Explain the study at an appropriate level, negotiate access, and build rapport through reliability, listening, participation, and reciprocal respect. Consent is ongoing and does not erase power differences.

## Participant observation

Observe while participating enough to understand practical knowledge. Record routine, exception, interaction, spatial arrangement, timing, objects, language, emotion, and the researcher's position. Alternate broad scans with focused observation.

## Interviewing

Use informal conversation for naturally arising meanings and formal interviews for deliberate depth and comparison. Ask open questions, follow participant terms, seek concrete episodes, probe contrasts, and test interpretations. Key informants are partners with partial positions, not cultural representatives by default.

## Additional guides

- checklists and matrices prevent systematic omissions;
- questionnaires provide limited comparable coverage;
- projective prompts can open indirect discussion but require contextual validation;
- documents, archives, and artifacts reveal official and material culture;
- unobtrusive measures capture traces without relying on self-report, but still require ethical scrutiny;
- proxemics and kinesics support analysis of space and bodily interaction without universalizing meanings.

Methods should converge on the question while preserving disagreement among evidence types.
