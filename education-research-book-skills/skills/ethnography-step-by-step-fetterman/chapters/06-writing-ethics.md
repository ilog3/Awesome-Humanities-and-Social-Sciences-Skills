# 记录奇迹，并轻轻走过田野

## Writing across the study

- **Proposal:** purpose, field, role, methods, access, analysis, ethics, products.
- **Fieldnotes:** detailed episodes plus methodological and reflexive context.
- **Memos:** emerging concepts, patterns, contradictions, and decisions.
- **Interim reports:** check direction, communicate with stakeholders, and expose premature conclusions.
- **Final product:** argument, thick evidence, interpretation, limits, and implications for its audience.

## Thick description

Distinguish a physical act from its cultural meaning—for example, an eyelid movement from a wink, parody, rehearsal, or involuntary twitch—by reconstructing context, intention, shared code, history, and interpretation. Use verbatim excerpts selectively and explain why they matter.

“Ethnographic present” can freeze a changing culture. Mark dates, trajectories, and historical conditions. Make ethnographic presence visible: how the researcher came to know, selected, and wrote the scene.

## Ethical lifecycle

Ethics runs from funding and topic selection through access, field relations, analysis, publication, archiving, and later reuse. Academic and applied ethnography share duties, but applied work adds sponsors, deadlines, organizational politics, proprietary claims, and policy effects.

Refuse projects whose sponsor predetermines conclusions. Clarify control of data and publication, conflicts of interest, participant risk, and whether findings may be used for discipline or exclusion. Anonymization may fail in small groups; alter or withhold detail when necessary and disclose analytic costs.

## Exit and accountability

Plan how relationships end or continue, how findings return to participants, and how errors can be corrected. “Do no harm” is necessary but insufficient; weigh voice, benefit, reciprocity, and the unequal ability to contest the published account.
