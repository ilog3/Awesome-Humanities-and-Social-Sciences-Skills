# 走出森林之路：分析

## Iterative reflection

Analysis is continuous reflection on observations, conversations, records, and the researcher's changing understanding. Write analytic memos early and let them guide purposive follow-up.

## Triangulation

Compare methods, sources, investigators, times, places, and theories. Convergence increases confidence; divergence identifies multiple realities, role effects, temporal change, or measurement problems. Do not force reconciliation.

## Patterns and key events

Patterns are recurring relationships among action, meaning, setting, and consequence. Test recurrence, distribution, exceptions, and rival explanations. Key events condense cultural organization—conflict, ceremony, policy change, breakdown—but must be situated against ordinary baseline life.

## Visual and formal aids

- maps show spatial access, boundaries, movement, and symbolic geography;
- flowcharts show process, decision, and temporal sequence;
- organization charts show formal structure and invite comparison with actual influence;
- network diagrams show relationships, brokers, clusters, and exclusions;
- content analysis identifies recurring forms and meanings in text or media;
- simple statistics clarify frequency, distribution, or contrast inside the case.

## Crystallization

Crystallization combines different angles and genres to render a complex object rather than claiming that all evidence converges on one truth. End with a claim table: evidence, counterevidence, participant interpretation, researcher interpretation, confidence, and needed follow-up.
