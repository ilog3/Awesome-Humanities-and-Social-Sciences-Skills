# Mixed Design, Analysis, and Reporting

## Mixed process

1. Determine whether linked questions truly need both approaches.
2. State the rationale: complementarity, development, expansion, initiation, or triangulation.
3. Select or construct sequence, priority, points of interface, and sampling relationship.
4. Generate component data with method-specific quality controls.
5. Analyze components appropriately.
6. Integrate through connecting, building, merging, embedding, data transformation, joint displays, or mixed-analysis matrices.
7. Validate components and integrated inference continuously.
8. Explain convergence, complementarity, and contradiction; report limitations and participant burden.

## Analysis

Quantitative analysis begins with tables/graphs, center, variability, relative position, cross-tabs, association/regression, then uses sampling distributions, interval estimation, hypothesis testing, t tests, ANOVA, chi-square, and related models where justified. Report effect and uncertainty.

Qualitative analysis begins during collection: interim analysis, memos, visual data inspection, secure storage, segmentation, inductive and a priori coding, co-occurrence and contrast, hierarchical categories, relationships, diagrams, and confirmation through contrary evidence and reflexivity.

## Reporting

Use current outlet standards. Quantitative reports make design, variables, sample, measurement, analysis, estimates, uncertainty, and deviations explicit. Qualitative reports show setting, relations, evidence, interpretation, voice, reflexivity, and transfer boundary. Mixed reports make the interface visible and give the integrated conclusion more than a final paragraph.
