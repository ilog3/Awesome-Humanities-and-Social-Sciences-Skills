# Foundations, Measurement, and Sampling

Move between induction, deduction, exploration, and verification. Judge explanations by empirical support, testability, parsimony without oversimplification, breadth, precision, and usefulness.

For planning, synthesize literature, state question/hypothesis, define constructs and operations, select a design, anticipate ethics, and specify analysis before seeing outcomes.

Measurement requires an interpretation/use argument: construct definition, content sampling, standardized administration where appropriate, scoring, reliability/precision, content and construct evidence, criterion relations, fairness, and consequences. For questionnaires, pilot wording, order, format, recall, response options, accessibility, mode, and nonresponse.

Choose tests, questionnaires, interviews, focus groups, observations, or existing data according to evidence needed and known error. Multiple sources can complement or contradict; do not assume agreement.

Quantitative sampling requires target population, frame, probability mechanism, sample size/precision, clustering, coverage, response, and weights. Qualitative sampling uses purposive cases for depth, variation, typicality, intensity, theory development, or negative evidence. Mixed sampling must state how component samples relate: identical, nested, parallel, or multilevel.

Gatekeeper access never replaces voluntary informed consent. Use current safeguards for children, dependent relationships, sensitive questions, confidentiality, data linkage, and digital storage.
