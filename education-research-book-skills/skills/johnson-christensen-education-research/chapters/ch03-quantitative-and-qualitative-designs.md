# Quantitative and Qualitative Designs

## Quantitative

- **Experiment:** manipulate treatment and use random assignment; consider posttest/pretest control, factorial, repeated-measures, field/lab/online structure.
- **Quasi-experiment:** use nonequivalent groups, interrupted time series, regression discontinuity, or another defensible counterfactual when random assignment is unavailable.
- **Single-case:** use repeated observations and A–B–A/A–B–A–B, multiple baseline, or changing criterion logic; address stability, autocorrelation, carryover, irreversibility, and generality.
- **Nonexperimental:** descriptive, predictive, explanatory, cross-sectional, longitudinal, retrospective, correlational, or causal-comparative designs; use matching, restriction, design, and statistical adjustment cautiously.

## Qualitative

- **Phenomenology:** structure and meaning of lived experience; show variation and researcher interpretation.
- **Ethnography:** culture and patterned life through sustained contextual participation and multiple sources.
- **Case study:** bounded case, within-case depth, embedded units, and analytic transfer.
- **Grounded theory:** iterative collection, constant comparison, coding, memoing, theoretical sampling, and integrated process explanation.
- **Historical research:** primary/secondary evidence, external/internal criticism, chronology, context, and rival interpretation.

Each tradition changes the question, sample, researcher role, analysis, and report. Do not select the label after generic data collection.
