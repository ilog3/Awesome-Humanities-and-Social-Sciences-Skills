# Quantitative–Qualitative–Mixed Cheatsheet

## Select

- Variables, prevalence, relationship, effect → quantitative.
- Meaning, experience, culture, case, process → qualitative.
- Linked questions and an actual integration decision → mixed.

## Audit

1. What claim and evidence?
2. Which paradigm assumptions?
3. How are participants/cases selected?
4. How is the construct or phenomenon represented?
5. What alternative explanation or reading is strongest?
6. Which validity, credibility, or legitimation threat matters most?
7. Where does integration happen?
8. What ethical burden and data risk arise?
9. What magnitude, uncertainty, contradiction, and limit remain?
10. What may not be concluded?

## Red flags

- “The study proves” → state evidence and confidence.
- “Mixed methods means stronger” → show integration and legitimation.
- “Control variables remove confounding” → state assumptions and residual bias.
- “Participants validated the theme” → treat response as one source.
- “Saturation was reached” → define domain, evidence, and stopping logic.
- “APA requires this” → verify current edition and outlet.

## Currency warning

The source's English edition is 2012 and Chinese edition 2015. Update ethics, statistics, measurement, digital research, data governance, open science, and reporting guidance.
