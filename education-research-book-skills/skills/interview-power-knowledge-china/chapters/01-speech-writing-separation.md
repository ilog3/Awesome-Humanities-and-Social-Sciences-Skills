# 言文分离：从“甲板／加班”进入问题

## Central contrast

The essay begins from a written joke in which characters chosen to imitate dialectal sound (“甲板”) simultaneously evoke an unrelated lexical meaning, while normalized characters (“加班”) preserve meaning but erase the comic voice. This is not just an anecdote; it is a diagnostic device for asking whether writing can transparently carry speech.

Chinese characters can represent sound, but ordinary reading gives semantic recognition priority. When sound imitation interrupts meaning, the material form of writing becomes conspicuous. The essay calls this everyday tension **言文分离** and contrasts it with traditions that treat alphabetic writing primarily as a representation of speech.

## Analytic questions

- What does the participant's utterance mean lexically?
- How exactly did it sound, and to whom did that sound matter?
- What social identity, intimacy, place, memory, or humor is indexed by pronunciation?
- Does a standard transcript silently convert a situated voice into standardized prose?
- Would a phonetic transcript become unreadable or produce a different social distance?

## Use without overclaiming

Use the contrast as a sensitivity exercise, not a rigid binary. Chinese writing has phonetic resources and alphabetic writing also has conventions, ambiguity, and normalization. The claim is about dominant tendencies and methodological blind spots, not linguistic destiny.

## Practical representation

For meaning-bearing pronunciation, keep layered records:

1. audio/video timestamp;
2. close orthographic rendering;
3. pronunciation aid such as pinyin, IPA, or locally intelligible notation where needed;
4. semantic gloss;
5. field note explaining why the form mattered in that interaction.

Never present one layer as the untouched original.
