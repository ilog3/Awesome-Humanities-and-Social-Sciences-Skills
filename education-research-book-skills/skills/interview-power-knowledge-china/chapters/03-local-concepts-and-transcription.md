# 专有名称、本土概念与转录

## Why names matter

A proper name can be inseparable from a remembered voice. Pronouncing a childhood acquaintance's name in standardized speech may feel as though it refers to someone else. The name therefore carries more than dictionary meaning: it indexes a relationship, locality, biography, and embodied memory.

The essay links this specificity to **本土概念**. A local concept is not merely an exotic synonym. It is a term participants use to organize their world, often with a restricted range of users, situations, pronunciations, and implied relations.

## Transcription is analysis

Every transcript makes decisions about:

- standardizing dialect, grammar, and word choice;
- marking pauses, overlap, emphasis, pace, and volume;
- naming speakers and places;
- translating or glossing local expressions;
- omitting interaction judged irrelevant;
- replacing uncertainty with a confident-looking phrase.

These decisions create the object later coded as “data.” Keep an audit trail instead of claiming neutrality.

## Layered transcript protocol

| Layer | Purpose | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| Encounter record | Preserve event | timestamp, setting, participants, interruption |
| Close transcript | Preserve form | pauses, overlap, dialectal wording, uncertainty |
| Reading transcript | Enable access | standardized characters and punctuation |
| Concept memo | Preserve situated meaning | usage conditions, speaker explanation, contrasts |
| Analytic memo | Build interpretation | competing readings and evidence links |

Pair pronunciation with an accessible gloss. Use pseudonyms and redaction where identification creates risk; preservation of sound never overrides consent or confidentiality.

## Guardrail

Vocal form may carry meaning, but it does not interpret itself. Do not infer class, personality, authenticity, or a whole cultural rhythm from accent or elongated speech without contextual and comparative evidence.
