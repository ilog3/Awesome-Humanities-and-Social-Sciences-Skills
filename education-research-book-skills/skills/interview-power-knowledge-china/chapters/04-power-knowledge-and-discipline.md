# 方法话语中的权力—知识

## Method is not outside history

The essay treats in-depth interviewing as a historically situated knowledge practice that arose within particular linguistic, philosophical, and institutional conditions. Once presented as a universal route to truth, a local method can hide its own assumptions.

Analyze the method as **discourse**:

- What counts as a valid research problem?
- What form of evidence is recognizable?
- Which training and vocabulary confer expertise?
- Who may speak, transcribe, interpret, and publish?
- Which phenomena disappear because the method cannot readily represent them?

## Power—knowledge

Power and knowledge are mutually constitutive, not merely an external authority distorting otherwise free knowledge. A knowledge field produces categories, objects, competent subjects, and permissible statements. Its productive power works through researchers' voluntary, skilled practice as much as through prohibition.

Apply this idea to interviewing by tracing:

`method textbook → training norm → interview conduct → transcript convention → analytic category → publishable claim → recognized researcher`

## Discipline / 规训

The double sense of “discipline” is analytically useful: a field of knowledge and a technology of training, correction, and self-control. Educational institutions are central sites where these senses combine. Ask how methodological competence disciplines researchers and how research categories reshape those being studied.

## Cultural-hegemony test

The essay provocatively calls speech-centered assumptions **音声帝国主义**. Use the term only after showing a mechanism: speech is ranked closer to truth, writing is treated as secondary representation, and a method built on that ranking is universalized in a writing-centered context.

Do not reduce intellectual exchange to domination. Also record what imported qualitative methods enabled, which local traditions they challenged, and how researchers adapt them.
