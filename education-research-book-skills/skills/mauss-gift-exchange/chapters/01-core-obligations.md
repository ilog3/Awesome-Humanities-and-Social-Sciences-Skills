# 核心机制：总体呈献与三重义务

## Apparently voluntary, effectively obligatory

Mauss studies exchanges presented as generous and free but structured by enforceable expectations and interests. The unit is often **prestation**—a service, performance, feast, ritual, person, privilege, or object—not a commodity alone. Whole collectivities commit themselves through representatives.

## Three obligations

1. **Give:** demonstrate generosity, open relations, claim honor, recognize or challenge another party.
2. **Receive:** accept relationship, respect, alliance, and often risk; refusal may signal hostility or inferiority.
3. **Return:** answer the prior gift and restore or transform relative standing; failure can bring shame, spiritual danger, exclusion, or loss of authority.

These are rights as well as duties. A party's duty to give may answer another's claim to hospitality; a duty to return creates a new right to receive.

## Hau and attached things

In Mauss's reading of Māori accounts, the **hau** of the thing seeks a route back toward its source. The analytic lesson is that a transferred object can remain connected to its giver and place. Giving something may be giving something of oneself; receiving incorporates part of another person and creates responsibility.

Use this as an emic-theoretical clue. Ask what attachment is locally recognized—ancestry, authorship, honor, memory, sacredness, relationship, data provenance—without imposing “hau” on every case.

## Circulation map

Do not stop at A gives B and B repays A. Follow third parties, delayed returns, descendants, gods, ancestors, political offices, and redistributive centers. The return may be a different object or a service of higher value, and its timing can sustain the relationship.
