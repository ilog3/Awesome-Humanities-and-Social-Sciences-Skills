# 总体社会事实与比较方法

## Total social fact

A gift system can activate multiple institutions at once. It is simultaneously:

- legal: duties, rights, sanctions, property, contract;
- economic: value, wealth, production, consumption, credit, redistribution;
- religious: sacred force, sacrifice, ancestors, taboo;
- moral: honor, generosity, trust, shame;
- political and familial: rank, alliance, lineage, representation;
- aesthetic: display, performance, crafted valuables, feasts;
- morphological: assemblies, routes, seasons, markets, demographic grouping.

“Total” does not mean mentioning everything. It means reconstructing how these dimensions work together in a concrete social movement.

## Comparative method

Mauss restricts comparison to documented cases whose vocabularies and institutions can be interpreted, then places them side by side to identify recurrent mechanisms and historically specific forms. Reproduce that discipline:

1. retain local terms and usage;
2. reconstruct each system before abstracting;
3. compare functions and relations, not surface objects alone;
4. track contradictory evidence and limits;
5. recompose legal, economic, religious, and political fragments into lived wholes.

## Evidence discipline

The essay relies heavily on early ethnographies, translations, legal texts, and philology. Some classifications and colonial-era descriptions are dated or contested. Verify claims with current scholarship when accuracy matters, distinguish Mauss's inference from the cited observers' report, and avoid reproducing civilizational hierarchies.
