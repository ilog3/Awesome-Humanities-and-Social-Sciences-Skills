# 道德与政治经济结论

## Mauss's normative move

Mauss argues that modern societies still expect generosity, reciprocity, occupational solidarity, social insurance, and responsible wealth. Humans are neither purely selfless nor purely calculating; institutions can combine individual interest with group security and moral obligation.

Potential applications include mutual aid, social protection, cooperatives, professional associations, fair labor relations, redistribution, hospitality, and public recognition. The normative center is not nostalgic return to an archaic order but a social economy in which wealth circulates and dignity is reciprocally recognized.

## Peace through exchange

Groups facing one another may avoid, fight, or establish relations. Giving, receiving, and returning can replace weapons with alliance and allow parties to remain distinct without mutual destruction. This peace is made through rules, ritual, interest, and restraint—not spontaneous harmony.

## Design questions

- Does the institution let people give without sacrificing subsistence?
- Can recipients refuse without exclusion or humiliation?
- Is return flexible and collective, or is gratitude converted into control?
- Are hidden contributions recognized?
- Does redistribution reduce insecurity or reproduce patronage?
- Are shared resources governed as a common table rather than captured by a superior seat?

## Caution

Mauss's policy conclusions are historically situated and partly programmatic. Use them to surface neglected moral and relational dimensions of economics; do not treat ethnographic analogy as sufficient evidence for a contemporary intervention.
