# 夸富宴：荣誉、竞争、信用

## Agonistic prestation

The potlatch of the Northwest Coast intensifies total prestation into competitive generosity. Chiefs and groups feast, give, repay with increase, display wealth, or destroy valuables to assert rank and place rivals under obligation. Peaceful exchange contains struggle; generosity can dominate.

## Honor and face

The stake is not merely property but name, ancestors, titles, masks, dances, privileges, and public recognition. Witnesses and ritual make claims socially durable. An inadequate return may mean losing face, rank, or the authority to speak for a group.

## Credit and time

Gift exchange demonstrates that credit and obligation can precede or exceed coined money and spot barter. Delay is productive: it keeps the relation alive, permits accumulation, and creates trust or dependence. Interest-like escalation may be expressed as honor rather than price.

## Destruction and consumption

Burning, breaking, or lavishly consuming wealth may prove the giver's capacity and prevent simple economic recovery. Analyze destruction as public communication and political action, while also recording material loss and coercive pressure.

## Application guardrail

Do not celebrate competitive giving as community by definition. Ask who finances prestige, who performs hidden labor, who is unable to reciprocate, and whether the system converts vulnerability into lasting subordination.
