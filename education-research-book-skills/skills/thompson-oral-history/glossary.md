# Glossary

- **Oral history:** Recorded interviewing and critical interpretation of spoken recollections as historical evidence.
- **Oral tradition:** Knowledge transmitted across people and generations; distinct from an interview about personal experience.
- **Narrator:** Person giving an oral-history account; emphasizes authorship and agency.
- **Life history:** Extended account organized around the course and meaning of a life.
- **Topic guide:** Flexible map of subjects and prompts, not a rigid questionnaire.
- **Source criticism:** Evaluation of provenance, position, production, consistency, corroboration, bias, and limits.
- **Retrospective reconstruction:** Present reorganization of past experience through later knowledge and identity.
- **Telescoping:** Compressing or merging events and time periods in recall.
- **Collective memory:** Socially shared frameworks through which groups remember.
- **Representativeness:** Warranted relation between narrators and a wider population, experience, or social position.
- **Theoretical sampling:** Selecting cases to develop categories, mechanisms, differences, and explanations.
- **Preservation master:** Protected archival copy from which use copies are derived.
- **Time-coded index:** Outline connecting topics and events to exact points in a recording.
- **Co-production:** Account shaped by narrator, interviewer, relationship, setting, questions, and editing.
- **Narrative form:** Plot, sequence, genre, metaphor, and performance that make experience meaningful.
- **Return to community:** Giving recordings, findings, access, products, or interpretive authority back.
