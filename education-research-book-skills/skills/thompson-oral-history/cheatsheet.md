# Oral History Cheatsheet

## Before

- Define intervention and audience.
- Inventory sources and silences.
- Match sampling to inference.
- Prepare guide, consent choices, metadata, and repository.
- Test equipment and file naming.

## During

- Confirm consent and attribution.
- Invite narrative before drilling down.
- Ask one clear question at a time.
- Probe incidents, sequence, roles, change, and meaning.
- Mark firsthand, secondhand, and uncertain material.
- Respect distress, silence, and boundaries.
- Confirm access and follow-up.

## Immediately after

- Write a field memo.
- Create preservation and working copies.
- Complete metadata and restrictions.
- Note post-interview comments separately.
- List corroborating sources and unresolved questions.

## Analysis and publication

- Preserve whole-life context.
- Test internal and external consistency.
- Examine memory processes and narrative form.
- Seek contradictions and negative cases.
- Distinguish fact from meaning claims.
- State sample reach and absences.
- Trace every excerpt to media and permission.
- Disclose editing and translation.
- Return usable results to participants or community.

## Red flags

- “The recording speaks for itself.”
- “Written sources are objective.”
- “These vivid stories prove prevalence.”
- “Silence proves repression.”
- “Consent to record means consent to every future use.”
- “A clean transcript is equivalent to the encounter.”
