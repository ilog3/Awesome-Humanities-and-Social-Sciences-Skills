# Preservation, Transcription, and Rights

## Evidence package

Keep linked records for preservation-master media, working and access copies, descriptive and technical metadata, consent and restriction terms, field memo, guide version, transcript or time-coded index, corrections, related objects, provenance, repository accession, and access history.

Use current archival formats and repository guidance. The book's tape-specific instructions express durable principles—quality, master/use separation, labeling, safe storage, and discoverability—not a requirement to use historical media.

## Transcription choices

A transcript transforms speech. Decide whether the purpose requires verbatim features, readable edited prose, a time-coded index, or multiple versions. Document treatment of dialect, fillers, pauses, overlap, inaudible words, translation, names, and sensitive passages. Check quotations against audio.

Never discard the recording because a transcript exists. Voice, pacing, interaction, and uncertainty remain evidence.

## Rights and access

Recording ownership, copyright, privacy, data protection, defamation, community protocols, and repository terms vary by jurisdiction and time. Use current legal and institutional guidance rather than the book's historical legal discussion.

Consent is granular: recording, deposit, public access, quotation, broadcast, online publication, reuse, and commercial use may require distinct choices. Explain future discoverability and track restrictions and expiry dates.

## Editorial integrity

Editing can improve clarity or fabricate meaning through rearrangement. Preserve a traceable path from every public excerpt to the original. Disclose substantive cuts, composites, translations, reordered material, and voice alterations.
