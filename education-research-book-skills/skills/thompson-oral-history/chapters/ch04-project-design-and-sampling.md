# Project Design and Sampling

## Design backward from use

Specify the historical product and audience before collecting. A scholarly comparison, family archive, museum installation, school project, community book, and public audio series require different sampling, rights, metadata, technical quality, and editorial processes.

Build a design memo containing the central problem, time span, unit of analysis, existing sources, archival gaps, narrator criteria, sampling route, stopping logic, community roles, resources, repository, access policy, outputs, and return to participants.

## Sampling options

- **Random:** when a reliable frame exists and population estimates matter.
- **Quota/stratified:** cover dimensions such as occupation, neighborhood, generation, gender, or institutional position.
- **Community-stratified:** map internal social sections before recruitment.
- **Network/snowball:** reach connected or hard-to-locate groups; record network bias.
- **Key informant:** select unusually relevant knowledge; supports depth, not prevalence.
- **Theoretical:** add cases to refine categories, mechanisms, differences, and explanations.

Retrospective populations are altered by mortality, migration, and willingness. Map missing categories and recruit beyond the easiest network.

## Pilot and iteration

Pilot the topic guide, consent explanation, recording arrangement, and metadata form. Review early interviews for missing categories, leading questions, poor audio, and emerging rival accounts. Revise prospectively and retain a change log.
