# Achievements and Evidence

## Distinct contributions

Oral evidence can open subjects where records are scarce, shift viewpoint from institutions to lived experience, connect domestic and public life, reveal informal practices, preserve narrative language and emotion, and challenge established explanations. Do not reduce it to colorful quotations for an account decided in advance.

## Source criticism

For each claim ask:

1. What could this narrator directly observe?
2. What is firsthand, secondhand, inferred, or retrospectively interpreted?
3. How far is the event from the interview in time?
4. What personal, social, or public narratives organize recall?
5. How did interviewer, setting, relationship, and recording shape the account?
6. Is the chronology internally coherent?
7. Which parts agree with or conflict with documents, material evidence, and other accounts?
8. Does disagreement indicate error, different social position, or contested meaning?

## Reliability and representativeness

Reliability is not a single property of a whole narrator. Someone may remember work routines well but dates poorly, or convey institutional atmosphere while misidentifying a formal title. Calibrate claims item by item.

Representativeness depends on purpose. Random or stratified samples support some distributional claims; theoretical or key-informant samples clarify mechanisms and meanings. Attrition, migration, mortality, willingness, and survival reshape any retrospective sample. State the target population and route from sample to inference.

## Triangulation matrix

For each key claim record: testimony segment, claim type, narrator position, corroborating source, conflicting source, plausible memory process, and confidence level. Preserve contradictions instead of resolving them by preference.
