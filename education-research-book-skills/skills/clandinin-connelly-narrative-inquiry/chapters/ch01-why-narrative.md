# Chapter 1: 为什么要叙事？

## Core idea

Narrative is a way of understanding experience because human lives unfold through time, relationships, actions, and changing situations. It is simultaneously what is studied and how inquiry proceeds.

## Frameworks introduced

- **Deweyan experience**
  - **Continuity:** every experience grows from prior experience and alters later possibilities.
  - **Interaction:** experience forms through a person's relation with social and material conditions.
- **Narrative knowing:** use when meaning depends on sequence, intention, consequence, identity, and context rather than category membership alone.
- **Narrative as phenomenon and method:** study stories people live and tell while narratively examining how those stories are composed and changed.

## Mental models

Think of experience as a moving current, not a set of frozen events. Think of a story as an interpretation of that current, addressed to someone under present conditions. The same episode may be retold differently without one version automatically being deceitful.

## Worked example

A teacher says, “I became strict after that class failed.” A categorical analysis might code strictness and failure. Narrative inquiry reconstructs earlier teaching ideals, the failure episode, institutional judgment, later classroom practices, anticipated future identity, and the present relationship in which the teacher tells the story.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating story as decorative quotation around an already fixed theory.
- Assuming a coherent narrated self existed unchanged before the interview.
- Extracting an episode from its temporal and relational movement.

## Key takeaways

1. Begin with experience rather than variables alone.
2. Preserve sequence, sociality, and situation.
3. Include the research encounter in the account of how a story became knowable.
