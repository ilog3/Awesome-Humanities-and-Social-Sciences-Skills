# Chapter 8: 从现场文本到研究文本

## Core idea

The move to research text is a difficult interpretive transition: experience must become meaningful to readers without being reduced to a theme list or forced into a conventional research plot.

## Three justifications

Continuously answer:

- **Personal:** why this inquiry matters to the researcher and their life.
- **Practical:** how it matters to the practice and people involved.
- **Social/theoretical:** how it joins a broader scholarly and public conversation.

## Research puzzle

A narrative inquiry begins with a puzzle—an unsettled curiosity grounded in experience. Refine it by asking what phenomenon is being explored, why it matters, and how narrative inquiry makes it accessible. The puzzle may change; document the change.

## Interpretive movements

1. Return to field texts in three dimensions.
2. Identify recurring **narrative threads**, tensions, silences, turning points, and counterstories.
3. Test candidate threads against the whole archive and participant responses.
4. Read relevant theory and literature as conversation, not predetermined codebook.
5. Experiment with text forms that can carry time, relation, and place.
6. Create a provisional **narrative unity** while leaving visible discontinuity and uncertainty.

## Worked example

Instead of reporting “three themes of teacher identity,” follow a narrative thread such as “being recognized as a real teacher” across childhood memories, training, a public lesson, conflict with a principal, and future plans. Show how different places and relationships change what recognition means.

## Key takeaways

1. Explanation, phenomenon, method, and theory must remain connected.
2. A narrative thread crosses texts and time; it is more than a frequent code.
3. Failed forms are normal evidence that the researcher is still finding the inquiry's shape.
