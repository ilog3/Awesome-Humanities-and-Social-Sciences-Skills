# Chapter 10: 持久注意的问题

## Core idea

Ethics is lived across the inquiry, not settled by initial consent. Narrative texts can alter identities, relationships, institutional reputations, and future stories long after fieldwork.

## Persistent issues

### Anonymity

Pseudonyms may protect but cannot reliably conceal a person in small communities. They may also deny authorship or relational recognition. Decide with attention to participant preference, third parties, deductive identification, future circulation, and the researcher's non-transferable duties.

### Ownership and relational responsibility

Ask who owns recordings, letters, photographs, interpretations, and the story created together; who may revise, withdraw, archive, quote, translate, or republish them; and what happens when researcher and participant interests diverge.

### How researchers are storied

Participants also tell stories about the researcher. Record assigned roles, expectations, disappointments, and power. Do not reserve interpretive authority only for the investigator.

### Fact and fiction

Narrative composition selects, orders, condenses, and reconstructs. Literary craft can convey experience, but invented events or composite persons must never be presented as observed fact. Label transformations and protect the evidence trail.

### Risk, danger, and misuse

Criticism can reduce people to examples of deficit, and even positive stories can expose vulnerability. Evaluate harm to participants, unnamed third parties, communities, and institutions under likely future readings.

## Wakefulness

Maintain **wakefulness**: sustained alertness to relationships, assumptions, narrative boundaries, ethical shifts, and the consequences of each textual choice. Use an ethics log throughout design, fieldwork, interpretation, publication, and archiving.

## Worked example

If a teacher wants her real name used but her story implicates a recognizable student, individual authorization is insufficient. Recompose or withhold details, consult safeguarding and ethics requirements, and explain the resulting interpretive limit.

## Key takeaways

1. Responsibility exceeds formal consent.
2. Relational ownership must include people indirectly storied.
3. Stay awake after publication; circulation creates new contexts and risks.
