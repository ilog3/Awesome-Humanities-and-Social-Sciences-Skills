# Chapters 5–6: 进入并置身现场

## Core idea

Entering a narrative field means entering ongoing stories and becoming part of them. Access is therefore a long relational negotiation rather than a one-time permission event.

## Living, telling, retelling, reliving

- **Living:** participants and researchers enact stories in ordinary life.
- **Telling:** they compose experience for a present audience.
- **Retelling:** inquiry reorganizes what can be said and understood.
- **Reliving:** changed interpretations can alter later action and identity.

Do not assume the cycle is therapeutic or beneficial. Retelling may create exposure, conflict, or institutional consequences.

## Four negotiations

1. **Relationships:** roles, closeness, boundaries, reciprocity, and change.
2. **Purposes:** what each person believes the inquiry is for.
3. **Transitions:** entry, shifting participation, leaving, and continuing contact.
4. **Usefulness/validity:** what would make the work meaningful and accountable to participants and scholarly readers.

## From involvement to observation

Move between full participation and cooler observation. Too much distance produces abstract accounts; unchecked immersion hides patterns and risks over-identification. Use fieldnotes, conversation with colleagues, temporal distance, and participant dialogue to shift perspective.

## Worked example

A researcher returns to a former school. Familiarity enables rapid trust but also assigns an old staff identity. The field plan should record which stories colleagues expect the researcher to endorse, which rooms and conversations remain closed, and how the return changes both the school story and the researcher's autobiographical story.

## Key takeaways

1. Renegotiate purposes and relationships when the study changes.
2. Treat felt disorientation as possible methodological evidence.
3. Record how presence reshapes what is lived and told.
