# Chapters 2–3: 在边界上叙事地思考

## Core idea

Narrative inquiry develops at unstable boundaries with reductionist and formalist research. Its task is not to reject analysis or theory, but to prevent them from erasing living persons, time, action, uncertainty, and context.

## Frameworks introduced

- **Reductionist boundary:** general categories, objectives, observable behaviors, or numbers may fragment an experiential whole.
- **Formalist boundary:** a prior theoretical scheme, detached researcher, and drive for certainty can determine what is allowed to count before inquiry begins.
- **Narrative balance:** hold theory and lived material in productive tension; let each revise the other.

## Boundary dimensions

Ask whether the design preserves:

- **temporality** rather than treating observations as timeless;
- **persons** rather than interchangeable role-bearers;
- **action** rather than behavior detached from intention and consequence;
- **uncertainty** rather than premature closure;
- **context** rather than an imagined view from nowhere;
- **researcher location** rather than concealed authorship.

## Worked example

An evaluation team categorizes teachers by a taxonomy of instructional behaviors. A narrative version follows one teacher's history with the taxonomy, an observed lesson, her response to feedback, institutional pressures, and subsequent revision. The taxonomy remains useful, but no longer defines the entire experience.

## Theory's place

Use theory to sharpen the research puzzle, notice narrative threads, and join scholarly conversation. Do not use it to replace participants with examples of a predetermined concept. A strong narrative text shows where theory illuminated, strained against, or was revised by experience.

## Key takeaways

1. Boundary tension is permanent and productive.
2. Narrative inquiry remains systematic while accepting ambiguity.
3. The researcher's life and conceptual commitments belong in the methodological account.
