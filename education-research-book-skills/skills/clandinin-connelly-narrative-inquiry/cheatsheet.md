# Narrative inquiry decision guide

## Start here

| Situation | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Experience unfolds across time and relationships | Narrative inquiry | A theme list with no plot or context |
| You need a fixed causal estimate | Another or mixed design | Claiming narrative alone identifies effect |
| The question comes from lived uncertainty | Research puzzle | Premature problem-solution closure |
| One account dominates | Interwoven field texts | Treating transcript as whole life |

## Three-dimensional check

- If an episode looks like a personality trait, move **outward** to relations and institutions.
- If context overwhelms the person, move **inward** to feelings, hopes, and moral commitments.
- If the account looks static, move **backward/forward**.
- If it could have happened anywhere, specify **place** and test whether location changes meaning.

## Field-to-text decision tree

1. Is the source's provenance and relationship clear?
   - No → annotate before interpreting.
   - Yes → locate it in three dimensions.
2. Does a candidate thread cross more than one text/time/place?
   - No → keep it provisional.
   - Yes → seek counterstories and silence.
3. Does the proposed form preserve evidence, voice, and temporal movement?
   - No → test another form.
   - Yes → run the ethics and reader check.

## Ethics triggers

- New intimate detail → renegotiate use.
- Real-name request → check third parties and future circulation.
- Composite or condensed scene → label the transformation.
- Participant objects → document disagreement; do not claim consensus.
- Small setting → assume deductive identification is possible.
- “Beautiful story” but weak evidence → do not let resonance substitute for accountability.

## Final smell test

If the researcher's presence disappears, the text is likely hiding authorship. If participants appear only as quotations supporting themes, it is likely formalist. If history and future vanish, it is temporally thin. If everyone agrees too neatly, search for relational pressure and counterstories.
