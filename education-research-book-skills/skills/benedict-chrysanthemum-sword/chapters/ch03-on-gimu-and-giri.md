# On, Gimu, and Giri

## Core idea

Benedict models social life through differentiated indebtedness and repayment rather than one undivided concept of duty.

## Conceptual ledger

- **On:** A received benefit or burden of indebtedness arising from dependence, favor, care, or authority.
- **Gimu:** Duties represented as unlimited and never fully repayable, such as obligations to parents or sovereign in the book's scheme.
- **Giri:** Bounded and socially enforceable obligations toward the world or one's name, often requiring exact reciprocation and reputation management.
- **Chu / ko:** Terms associated in the book with loyalty and filial obligation; interpret historically and linguistically.

## Worked example

A gift from a superior may not be experienced as free. Map the benefit, status difference, accepted obligation, expected form and timing of return, audience, and consequence of nonreturn. Then test whether “on/giri” adds explanatory value beyond workplace authority, contract, reciprocity, or personal affection.

## Mental model

Treat obligation as a multidimensional ledger: source, status, magnitude, repayability, time horizon, audience, and sanction. Do not translate every favor into debt mechanically.

## Failure modes

- Flattening on, gimu, and giri into “loyalty.”
- Ignoring whether actors themselves invoke these categories.
- Assuming obligation eliminates strategy, resentment, affection, or refusal.

## Key takeaways

1. Identify the specific kind and direction of obligation.
2. Relate moral debt to hierarchy and reputation.
3. Test translated terms against context and historical usage.
