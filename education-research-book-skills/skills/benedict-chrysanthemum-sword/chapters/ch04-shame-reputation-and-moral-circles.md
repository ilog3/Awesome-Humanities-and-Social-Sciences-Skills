# Shame, Reputation, and Moral Circles

## Core idea

The book emphasizes external judgment, reputation, and situation-specific obligations, but its famous shame/guilt contrast should be used as a limited analytic hypothesis, not a civilizational binary.

## Frameworks introduced

- **Giri to one's name:** Obligation to preserve or restore reputation after insult, failure, or public exposure.
- **Moral circles:** Conduct may be judged through distinct domains of obligation rather than one universal command.
- **Shame sanction:** Anticipated or actual social judgment shapes action where relevant audiences can evaluate conduct.

## Worked example

For an apparent overreaction to criticism, identify who witnessed it, which status claim was threatened, what repair options existed, and whether the action restored standing. Compare private criticism, anonymous criticism, and public insult. If behavior does not vary with audience, a reputation explanation weakens.

## Failure modes

- “Japanese people feel shame; Westerners feel guilt.”
- Treating face-saving as irrational without analyzing relational stakes.
- Explaining state violence through culture while ignoring institutions and coercion.

## Key takeaways

1. Name the audience and sanction.
2. Separate reputation repair from inner moral evaluation while allowing both.
3. Treat shame and guilt as coexisting processes whose salience varies.
