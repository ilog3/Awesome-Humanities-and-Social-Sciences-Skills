# Quality, Ethics, and Reporting

## Quality by claim

Ask whether evidence is adequate for the exact descriptive, relational, causal, interpretive, historical, comparative, evaluative, or action-oriented claim. Use design-specific standards rather than one universal checklist.

Cross-cutting tests include transparent procedures, data provenance, construct clarity, sampling fit, alternative explanations, negative evidence, researcher/research reactivity, uncertainty, sensitivity, fairness, and reproducibility or auditability where appropriate.

## Ethics

Protect voluntary informed consent/assent, guardian permission where appropriate without erasing child voice, privacy, confidentiality limits, data minimization/security, equitable selection, accessibility, proportionate risk, withdrawal, debriefing, reciprocity, conflicts of interest, and safeguarding. Reassess throughout the study and follow current review requirements.

## Inference ladder

`material/observation → processed result → description → association/interpretation → explanation/causal claim → transfer/generalization → recommendation`

Each step needs an additional warrant. Stop where the evidence stops.

## Report structure

Include problem and contribution; question and purpose; literature/conceptual frame; paradigm and researcher role; design and setting; participants/sample; measures/protocols; ethics; data and analysis; results with evidence and uncertainty; alternative explanations; limitations; implications; and enough appendices/materials for appraisal when lawful and ethical.

Do not conceal nulls, attrition, deviations, contradictions, researcher influence, adverse effects, or scope limits. Separate findings from recommendations and identify the values behind recommendations.
