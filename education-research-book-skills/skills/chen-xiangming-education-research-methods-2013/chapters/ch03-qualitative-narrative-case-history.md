# Qualitative, Narrative, Case, and Historical Research

## Qualitative inquiry

Use when meaning, process, interaction, context, or participant experience is central. Build a flexible but documented design; sample purposively; negotiate relationships; generate rich interview, observation, and material evidence; analyze concurrently; code and compare while preserving context; practice reflexivity; and bound transfer claims. Researcher influence is evidence to analyze, not something hidden by claims of neutrality.

## Narrative inquiry

Treat stories as situated constructions of experience shaped by time, social relations, place, audience, genre, memory, and the research encounter. Analyze what is told, how events are configured, turning points, plot, voice, silence, counterstory, and whose interests representation serves. Do not reduce a life to isolated themes or treat narrative as transparent fact. Negotiate anonymity, quotation, authorship, and relational responsibility.

## Case study

Define the bounded case, why it is a case, time/place/organization boundary, unit and embedded units. Use multiple evidence sources and maintain a chain from question to material to claim. Within-case explanation precedes cross-case comparison. Cases support analytic insight and transfer through mechanism and context, not statistical representativeness.

## Historical research

Define period and question; locate primary and secondary sources; verify provenance, authorship, date, audience, purpose, integrity, and transmission; compare independent accounts; analyze chronology, context, continuity, rupture, agency, structure, and plausible causation. Absence in an archive is not evidence of absence without preservation and selection analysis. Avoid presentism and inevitable-progress stories.

## Shared interpretive audit

Distinguish observed/archival evidence, participant or historical-actor meaning, researcher interpretation, theoretical explanation, value judgment, and transfer. Search for negative cases and alternative readings and show the evidentiary chain.
