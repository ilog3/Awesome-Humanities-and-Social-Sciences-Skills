# Action, Comparative, and Integrated Research

## Action research

The practitioner or affected group studies and improves practice through iterative cycles:

`identify/understand → plan → act → observe systematically → reflect/dialogue → revise`

Define participation, evidence, values, decision authority, risks, and improvement criteria. Keep teaching/service responsibility primary. Do not force positive findings or confuse routine adjustment with research; document the baseline, intervention logic, evidence, contrary outcomes, and changes in practitioner understanding and conditions.

## Comparative education

Specify the units, dimensions, time, and reason for comparison. Establish conceptual and measurement equivalence before using a common label. Describe cases in context, then compare and explain through mechanisms. Analyze history, governance, economy, culture, demography, language, institutions, policy transfer, and within-case diversity. Avoid national stereotypes, league-table causality, and importing a “best practice” without translation.

## Integrated/mixed approach

Use multiple approaches when linked questions need different evidence. Specify the paradigm stance, component questions, priority, sequence, samples, data, analyses, and exact integration point. Integrate through design, sampling, data generation, transformation, joint displays, explanation, or interpretation. Treat disagreement as a result to investigate rather than choosing the preferred component.

## Decision rule

Add a method only if it changes what can defensibly be concluded. More methods increase coordination, ethics, and inference burdens; they do not automatically improve quality.
