# Research Cycle and Philosophical Inquiry

## General cycle

`problem sensing → question and purpose → literature/conceptual frame → design → evidence → analysis → conclusion → report/use → new problem`

At each transition, state the warrant. A meaningful problem must be researchable, educationally consequential, ethically acceptable, feasible, and connected to existing knowledge.

## Literature review

Define concepts and date/language/source boundaries; construct search strings; search multiple appropriate databases; record queries and decisions; screen by criteria; assess provenance and quality; extract design, context, sample, findings, limits, and conflicts; synthesize by concept, mechanism, method, chronology, or debate. Do not write one-paragraph-per-source summaries without integration.

## Philosophical inquiry

Use philosophical methods throughout research, especially when the issue concerns meaning, justification, value, or assumptions.

- **Induction:** move from cases toward a provisional general claim; inspect selection and counterexamples.
- **Deduction:** derive implications from premises; test validity and premise truth separately.
- **Conceptual analysis:** identify uses, necessary distinctions, boundary cases, criteria, and implications.
- **Phenomenological analysis:** clarify structures of lived experience while documenting standpoint and variation.
- **Hermeneutic analysis:** interpret parts and whole, text and context, through a revisable circle.
- **Critical analysis:** expose power, ideology, exclusion, and conditions of possibility; do not replace evidence with denunciation.

For every philosophical conclusion show the question, definitions, premises, argument, alternatives, counterexample, and practical implication.
