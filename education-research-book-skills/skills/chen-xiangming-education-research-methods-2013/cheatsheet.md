# Education Research Methods Cheatsheet

## Fast design test

1. What exact educational problem and claim?
2. Which people, setting, time, and level?
3. Which paradigm and values?
4. What does existing research establish or dispute?
5. Which method can produce the necessary evidence?
6. Is sampling aligned with inference?
7. Are constructs, cases, or sources clearly bounded?
8. What is the strongest rival explanation or interpretation?
9. What ethical risk and burden arise?
10. What may be concluded—and what may not?

## Method traps

| Trap | Correction |
|---|---|
| Questionnaire equals survey quality | Audit frame, response, construct, mode, and error. |
| Reliable score is valid | Build an interpretation/use validity argument. |
| Pre/post gain proves effect | Add comparator and address causal threats. |
| Significant means important | Report magnitude, uncertainty, context, and cost. |
| Interview quote proves theme | Compare cases and preserve context. |
| One story represents experience | Analyze construction, relation, and variation. |
| Famous case proves a rule | Bound case and explain analytic transfer. |
| Archive is the past | Analyze provenance, preservation, silence, and selection. |
| Reflection alone is action research | Add evidence, action, cycles, and accountability. |
| Country rank explains a system | Reconstruct comparable mechanisms and context. |

## Minimum deliverable

- Aligned question, purpose, paradigm, and method
- Literature/conceptual synthesis
- Sampling and operationalization/case boundary
- Data and analysis protocol
- Quality and ethics plan
- Inference boundary
- Transparent report outline

## Currency warning

Use current standards for research ethics, privacy/security, accessibility, open science, statistical reporting, test validity, and mixed-method reporting. The textbook's worked examples and policy environment date from 2013.
