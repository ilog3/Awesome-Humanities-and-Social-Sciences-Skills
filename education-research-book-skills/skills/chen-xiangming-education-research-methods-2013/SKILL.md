# Education Research Methods (Chen Xiangming, 2013)

Use a practice-first sequence: define a worthwhile educational problem, choose the kind of claim required, align paradigm and method, produce evidence under explicit quality and ethics controls, and report only what the design warrants.

## Method router

| Needed claim | Primary route |
|---|---|
| Clarify a concept, value, premise, or argument | Philosophical inquiry |
| Estimate prevalence, distribution, attitudes, or relations | Survey and measurement |
| Estimate an intervention's causal effect | Experiment or strong quasi-experiment |
| Summarize samples or quantify uncertainty | Educational statistics |
| Understand meaning, experience, context, or process | Qualitative inquiry |
| Study experience through stories and their construction | Narrative inquiry |
| Explain a bounded contemporary case in depth | Case study |
| Reconstruct change and causation over time | Historical research |
| Improve practice through practitioner inquiry cycles | Action research |
| Explain educational similarities and differences across systems | Comparative education |
| Answer linked questions requiring different evidence | Integrated/mixed approach |

Read [chapters/ch01-research-cycle-and-philosophy.md](chapters/ch01-research-cycle-and-philosophy.md) before choosing. Methods may be combined only when each answers a defined part of the question.

## Workflow

1. **Specify problem and contribution.** Turn a topic into a bounded question; identify educational importance, novelty, feasibility, affected groups, and the claim needed.
2. **Review knowledge.** Search reproducibly, assess source quality, synthesize agreements, conflicts, mechanisms, contexts, and gaps; derive a conceptual framework rather than a bibliography list.
3. **Declare paradigm and logic.** State assumptions, values, unit, variables or concepts, and whether reasoning is deductive, inductive, abductive, interpretive, critical, or mixed.
4. **Select design.** Match question to the router and document why alternatives are insufficient.
5. **Operationalize and sample.** Define constructs, cases or population; choose sampling logic; plan instruments or protocols; protect inclusion and access.
6. **Plan quality and ethics before collection.** Anticipate bias, validity threats, uncertainty, power, consent, privacy, risk, and stopping rules.
7. **Collect and manage evidence.** Use standardized procedures where required and reflexive documentation where interaction matters; maintain provenance and secure data.
8. **Analyze according to design.** Read [ch02](chapters/ch02-survey-measurement-experiment-statistics.md) for quantitative routes and [ch03](chapters/ch03-qualitative-narrative-case-history.md) for interpretive routes.
9. **Integrate or act where appropriate.** Read [ch04](chapters/ch04-action-comparative-and-integration.md).
10. **Audit inference.** Separate observation, estimate, interpretation, causal claim, evaluation, transfer, and recommendation. Read [ch05](chapters/ch05-quality-ethics-and-reporting.md).
11. **Report transparently.** Connect each conclusion to evidence, analysis, assumptions, uncertainty, limitations, and educational implications.

## Required output

Provide problem, question, purpose, paradigm, claim type, conceptual framework, design rationale, unit and sampling, operational definitions or case boundary, data and analysis plan, validity/credibility threats, ethics, reporting structure, limitations, and decision rules.

Use [patterns.md](patterns.md), [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md), and [glossary.md](glossary.md).

## Guardrails

- Do not choose a method from fashion, convenience, software, or labels alone.
- Do not call philosophical assertion, experience summary, or literature compilation a completed empirical study.
- Do not infer causation from correlation or a simple pre/post change.
- Do not treat reliability as validity, statistical significance as practical importance, or a scale score as the construct itself.
- Do not claim representativeness without a defensible sampling frame and response analysis.
- Do not treat qualitative research as casual interviewing or claim statistical generalization from purposive cases.
- Do not call a teacher activity “action research” without systematic evidence, reflection, revision, and ethical responsibility.
- Do not compare countries as isolated national essences; test comparable concepts and historical contexts.
- Use current ethics review, child safeguarding, privacy/data-security, accessibility, statistical, and reporting standards; the source is a 2013 introductory textbook.
