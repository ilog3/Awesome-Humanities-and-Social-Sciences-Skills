# Glossary

- **Qualitative inquiry:** Situated interpretation of meaning, experience, practice, interaction, culture, or process using primarily nonstandardized evidence.
- **Paradigm:** Linked assumptions about reality, knowledge, values, and warranted inquiry.
- **Lifeworld:** Everyday world as lived and taken for granted by actors.
- **Hermeneutic circle:** Iterative interpretation between parts and the evolving understanding of the whole.
- **Tacit knowledge:** Competence or understanding that actors use without fully articulating it.
- **Symbolic interactionism:** View that meanings emerge and change through social interaction.
- **Purposive sampling:** Deliberate case selection for information, relevance, and variation.
- **Theoretical sampling:** Emerging-concept-led selection used to develop theory.
- **Thick description:** Contextually and interpretively rich account that supports understanding and transfer judgments.
- **Reflexivity:** Systematic examination of how researcher position and relationships shape knowledge.
- **Triangulation:** Deliberate comparison of different evidential angles; not necessarily a search for agreement.
- **Member feedback:** Participant response to selected records or interpretations, treated as additional situated evidence.
- **Audit trail:** Traceable record of data transformations and analytic decisions.
- **Credibility:** Plausibility and evidential support of an interpretation within its context.
- **Transferability:** Capacity for readers to judge relevance to another context from sufficient description.
- **Mixed methods:** Intentional integration of qualitative and quantitative strands into joint inference.
