# Research Design

Use a cyclical design: purposes, questions, conceptual context, methods, and quality checks interact throughout the study.

## Design sequence

1. Describe the phenomenon provisionally without defining away discovery.
2. Formulate open research questions tied to action, meaning, process, or context.
3. State theoretical, practical, and social purposes.
4. Review background knowledge as orientation, not an imposed answer.
5. Bound the case or field while distinguishing case, setting, participant, and unit of analysis.
6. Choose a purposive sampling logic.
7. Anticipate access, roles, reciprocity, and researcher–participant relations.
8. Select methods for complementary evidential functions.
9. Design credibility checks into collection and analysis.

Single cases can support intensive contextual explanation and analytic transfer, not automatic population generalization. Designs may change as the field reveals new actors or problems; document each substantive change and its implications.
