# Data Collection

## Sampling

Choose typical, extreme, maximum-variation, criterion, critical, homogeneous, snowball, opportunistic, confirming/disconfirming, or theoretical cases according to the claim. Record how access and refusal shape the sample. Stop by evidential sufficiency or category saturation appropriate to the design, not a magical number.

## Participant observation

Clarify participation role, entry, gatekeepers, observation focus, relationships, and exit. Write descriptive fieldnotes promptly, separate observation from inference, capture sequences and talk, and add analytic and reflexive memos.

## In-depth interviews

Prepare a flexible guide, begin broadly, follow participant terms, probe episodes and contrasts, avoid double and leading questions, and document setting and interaction. Interview accounts are situated productions, not direct windows into truth.

## Focus groups

Compose groups for purposeful interaction, create ground rules, invite quieter voices, manage dominance, and analyze both content and group process. Agreement produced in a group is not independent corroboration.

## Tradition-specific collection

Ethnography prioritizes sustained cultural participation; oral history connects memory to historical sources; narrative work preserves sequence and story form; grounded theory alternates collection with concept-led sampling; action research collects evidence inside cycles of planning, action, observation, and reflection.
