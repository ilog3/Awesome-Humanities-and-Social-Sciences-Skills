# Paradigms and Ethics

Qualitative inquiry is a diverse family, not one technique. Its common concerns include situated meaning, lived experience, interaction, process, context, researcher participation, flexible design, and interpretive claims.

## Orientations

- **Interpretive/constructivist:** understand how actors make and negotiate meanings in lifeworlds.
- **Critical:** examine how power, institutions, ideology, and inequality shape experience; connect knowledge to critique or change.
- **Post-positivist/realist uses:** use qualitative evidence for explanation while explicitly testing interpretations and limitations.
- **Pragmatic/practice:** judge inquiry partly by its capacity to clarify and improve action.

Hermeneutics supports moving between part and whole, prior understanding and revised interpretation. Tacit knowledge directs attention to what competent actors know but may not readily state. Symbolic interactionism treats language and meaning as social action, not a transparent report of an inner state.

## Ethics and politics

Ethics is continuous from topic choice to archiving and publication. Address informed and ongoing consent, privacy, confidentiality limits, role conflict, reciprocity, vulnerability, deception, institutional power, emotional harm, representation, withdrawal, and future reuse.

Research cannot be politically empty: choosing problems, categories, voices, and audiences has consequences. Reflexivity requires examining these consequences, not performing a ritual confession.
