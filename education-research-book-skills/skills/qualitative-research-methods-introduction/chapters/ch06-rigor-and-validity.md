# Rigor and Validity

Quality criteria vary with paradigm and purpose, but no stance removes the obligation to show why an interpretation deserves attention.

## Threats

Look for selective attention, leading relations, premature closure, elite or convenience sampling, missing negative cases, decontextualized quotations, undocumented transformations, overidentification, participant performance, and claims beyond the data.

## Strategies

- sustained engagement and persistent observation;
- triangulation across sources, methods, researchers, or theories;
- respondent feedback on specific facts or interpretations;
- negative-case and rival-explanation analysis;
- peer debriefing and analytic challenge;
- rich contextual description for transfer judgments;
- reflexive journals and decision logs;
- a traceable audit trail from sources to findings.

Triangulation may reveal different situated truths rather than convergence. Participant feedback is additional evidence shaped by timing, relationship, and stakes—not a final referendum. Match each strategy to a concrete threat.

Report credibility, dependability, confirmability, transferability, relevance, ethical integrity, and practical usefulness only where they fit the study's epistemology.
