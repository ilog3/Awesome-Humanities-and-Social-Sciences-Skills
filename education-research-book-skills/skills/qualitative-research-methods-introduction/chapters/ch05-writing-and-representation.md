# Writing and Representation

Qualitative writing does not merely report a reality already formed; selection, sequence, voice, terminology, quotation, and genre construct an account. Treat authorship as methodological and ethical work.

## Writing decisions

- Identify readers and what evidence they need.
- Choose chronological, episodic, case-based, thematic, or layered organization.
- Balance participant perspectives with contextual and analytic interpretation.
- Explain the researcher's position and consequential relationships.
- Use thick, relevant description rather than indiscriminate detail.
- Introduce quotations with context and analyze their evidential role.
- Use numbers only when their construction and denominator are clear.
- Protect identities without inventing coherence or erasing social location.

Reflexivity should illuminate how knowledge was produced. Too little hides influence; too much turns participants into scenery for the researcher's autobiography.

Draft from a claim–evidence–interpretation outline, revise for counterevidence and alternative readings, then audit whether the title, abstract, method, findings, and conclusion make claims at the same scale.
