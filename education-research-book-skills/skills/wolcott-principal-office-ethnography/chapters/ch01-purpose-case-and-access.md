# Purpose, Case, and Access

## Core idea

The study asks what one elementary principal actually does and how that conduct becomes culturally intelligible. The person is a route into the role and system, not a sample average.

## Frameworks introduced

- **Descriptive-first question:** Ask “What is happening?” before imposing effectiveness criteria.
- **Case boundedness:** Define person, role, school, district, period, and accessible life contexts.
- **Strategic ordinary case:** An apparently typical, experienced principal can reveal routine organization; “representative” remains an analytic judgment, not statistical proof.
- **Whole-context attention:** Include apparently peripheral family, community, colleague, and district relations when they shape work.

## Worked example

Wolcott narrowed a broad interest in educational administration to one principal he could shadow intensively. He selected for full-time administrative responsibility, one elementary school, career commitment, and a district granting meaningful autonomy. A current study should make gender-neutral criteria, document rejected cases, and state how selection shapes the phenomenon observed.

## Access protocol

Secure the principal's agreement without letting a superior compel participation. Then negotiate separately with staff, students, families, and meeting participants. Define sensitive encounters, recording rules, withdrawal, confidentiality, and what the principal may review.

## Anti-patterns

- Selecting a celebrated leader to confirm a model.
- Calling one case typical without comparative evidence.
- Assuming district authorization equals participant consent.
- Treating inaccessible records or meetings as if they do not exist.

## Key takeaways

1. Case selection is part of the argument.
2. “A principal” and “the principalship” are different claim levels.
3. Access is distributed across actors and settings.
