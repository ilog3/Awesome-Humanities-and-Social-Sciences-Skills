# Shadowing and Data Strategy

## Core idea

Shadowing captures the flow, interruption, mobility, and relational nature of administrative work. Complementary evidence should test and contextualize patterns discovered through immersion.

## Frameworks introduced

- **Shadow role:** Follow the leader across ordinary settings while remaining an identifiable researcher.
- **Whole-day sampling:** Observe beginnings, transitions, quiet periods, travel, after-hours meetings, and endings—not only scheduled decisions.
- **Timed interval record:** Periodically classify behavior and contact to test impressions about work distribution.
- **Multiple-perspective supplement:** Use documents, staff interviews, student accounts, questionnaires, and calendars for distinct evidential purposes.

## Worked example

Wolcott combined prolonged shadowing with two-hour observation blocks recording behavior and social activity at sixty-second intervals, documents, staff views, and student impressions. In a contemporary study, first observe openly; then design a limited interval scheme from emergent categories such as contact type, location, task switching, and discretion. Compare counts with narrative fieldnotes.

## Fieldnote fields

Time, location, actors, initiation, issue, exact interaction, interruption, artifact, decision, follow-up, observed affect, researcher position, and analytic question.

## Anti-patterns

- Counting before learning locally meaningful categories.
- Recording only office activity.
- Treating self-report as equivalent to observed work.
- Making the shadow invisible in the account.

## Key takeaways

1. Shadow across settings and rhythms.
2. Use structured observation after ethnographic familiarization.
3. Triangulate for perspective and contradiction, not forced agreement.
