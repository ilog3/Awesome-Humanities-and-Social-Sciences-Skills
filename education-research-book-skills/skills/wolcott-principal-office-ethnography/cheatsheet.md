# Principal-Ethnography Cheatsheet

| Situation | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership literature says what principals should do | Observe what one principal does first | Prescription hides actual work |
| One principal is selected | Bound the claim to person-role-site-period | One case is not an average |
| The principal grants access | Negotiate access with other actors too | Consent is distributed |
| Day appears fragmented | Follow issues across interruptions | Fragmentation may be coordination |
| Informal talk seems trivial | Record its information and consequences | Informal contact may accomplish core work |
| Observer impressions feel stable | Add timed intervals or another perspective | Focused evidence can test immersion |
| Principal explains a decision | Compare prior observation and others' accounts | Rationales are situated data |
| Routine preserves order | Analyze what and whom it maintains | Maintenance is not neutral |
| Writing turns evaluative | Separate description, analysis, interpretation, evaluation | Readers need the evidential path |

## Fast questions

- What is going on here?
- What did the principal actually do?
- Who initiated and who was affected?
- Was this formal, informal, recurring, or exceptional?
- Where is it in the annual cycle?
- What expectation did it transmit?
- What did my shadow presence change?
- Which claim belongs only to this case and era?
