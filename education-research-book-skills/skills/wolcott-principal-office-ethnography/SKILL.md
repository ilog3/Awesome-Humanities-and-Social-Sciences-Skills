# Wolcott's Principal-Office Ethnography

Begin with the deceptively simple ethnographic question: **What is going on here?** Describe and analyze a school leader's actual social behavior before evaluating what leadership should be.

## Core framework

### Actual work versus prescribed role

Administrative literature often starts from duties, competencies, or ideals. Wolcott shadows one principal to recover how the work is accomplished through interruptions, encounters, routines, judgment, visibility, relationships, and the school-year cycle.

### Person-in-role-in-context

Study neither an abstract “principalship” nor a private personality alone. Follow a particular person occupying a role in one school, district, family, professional network, and historical period.

### Description → analysis → cultural interpretation

1. Describe events and conduct densely enough that readers can orient themselves.
2. Analyze recurring activities, relations, rhythms, and categories.
3. Interpret how the principal participates in maintaining and transmitting the school's cultural and organizational system.

Do not rush directly to judgment or recommendations.

## Workflow

1. **Bound the case.** State why one principal, school, and period can illuminate the question and what cannot be generalized. Read [chapters/ch01-purpose-case-and-access.md](chapters/ch01-purpose-case-and-access.md).
2. **Negotiate shadowing.** Establish access with the principal, staff, students, parents, district personnel, and sensitive events; clarify researcher role and ongoing consent.
3. **Observe whole workdays and settings.** Follow office, corridors, classrooms, staff room, meetings, travel, community, and professional gatherings. Read [chapters/ch02-shadowing-and-data-strategy.md](chapters/ch02-shadowing-and-data-strategy.md).
4. **Separate formal from informal contact.** Analyze scheduled meetings and official decisions alongside doorway exchanges, coffee, phone calls, passing remarks, and routine trouble-shooting. Read [chapters/ch03-formal-informal-and-annual-work.md](chapters/ch03-formal-informal-and-annual-work.md).
5. **Track time and rhythm.** Map days, weeks, school terms, recurring ceremonies, deadlines, crises, and unfinished tasks.
6. **Study system maintenance.** Identify how the principal is socialized into expectations and how the principal socializes staff, students, parents, and newcomers. Read [chapters/ch04-system-maintenance-and-socialization.md](chapters/ch04-system-maintenance-and-socialization.md).
7. **Add focused evidence after immersion.** Use interval observation, documents, staff interviews, student accounts, questionnaires, or simple counts to test patterns, not replace ethnography. Read [chapters/ch05-analysis-writing-and-adequacy.md](chapters/ch05-analysis-writing-and-adequacy.md).
8. **Audit observer effects and exclusions.** Record what the “shadow” changes, what cannot be observed, whose perspective dominates, and how gendered or institutional assumptions structure the case.
9. **Write descriptively before normatively.** Let readers see ordinary work and cultural logic; place evaluation in a separate, evidence-linked section.

## Required outputs

For a school-leadership ethnography provide case rationale, role and access agreements, site/actor map, shadowing schedule, event and time samples, fieldnote scheme, complementary evidence, formal–informal encounter matrix, annual-cycle map, system-maintenance analysis, reflexive limitations, and writing plan.

For analysis use [patterns.md](patterns.md); for rapid fieldwork use [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md); for terms use [glossary.md](glossary.md).

## Guardrails

- Do not equate “what occurs” with “what ought to occur.”
- Do not infer effectiveness from busyness, visibility, popularity, or conformity.
- Do not let the principal's account define the whole school.
- Do not assume one representative case statistically represents principals.
- Do not erase researcher effects, friendship, restricted access, or unobserved private life.
- Do not reproduce the source's male-default principal selection in contemporary design.
- Do not use staff or student participation as if the principal alone could consent for them.
