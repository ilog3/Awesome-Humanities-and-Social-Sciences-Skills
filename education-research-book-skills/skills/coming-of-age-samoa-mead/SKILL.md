## Core model

The book asks whether adolescence is necessarily a period of crisis. Analyze outcomes as an interaction:

`biological maturation × cultural expectations × available roles × family/peer relations × choice burden × individual history`

Cross-cultural comparison does not prove that biology is irrelevant. It can show that institutions organize the meaning, timing, intensity, and consequences of maturation differently.

## Working procedure

1. State the supposedly universal claim about adolescence or education.
2. Identify the social arrangements that make it appear natural in the focal society.
3. Reconstruct the contrasting arrangements described in the Samoan case.
4. Trace mechanisms through child-rearing, household, work, peers, sexuality, ritual, personality expectations, and life-course transitions.
5. Examine ordinary cases and conflict cases separately.
6. Compare choice load, emotional exclusivity, sanctions, role clarity, and exposure to birth, death, work, and sexuality.
7. Derive an educational hypothesis, not a direct policy import.
8. Audit the evidence against design limitations and later controversy.

## Decision rules

- Treat “adolescent storm and stress” as a question, not a natural-law premise.
- Do not replace biological determinism with total cultural determinism.
- Do not romanticize the Samoan society or treat it as internally uniform, timeless, or conflict-free.
- Distinguish what Mead observed, what participants reported, and what she inferred comparatively.
- A cultural practice can reduce one tension while creating gender, hierarchy, violence, or conformity costs elsewhere.
- Use current, local evidence before applying any educational conclusion.
- “Education for choice” means building capacity and coherent conditions for choice, not maximizing options without support.
- Always disclose that the fieldwork and interpretations became the subject of major methodological debate.

## Preferred output

Return: **universalized assumption → cultural arrangements → Samoan contrast → proposed mechanism → variation/conflict cases → methodological caveat → educational hypothesis → local evidence needed**.
