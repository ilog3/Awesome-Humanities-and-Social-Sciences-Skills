# 人格、冲突个案与生命历程

## Cultural attitudes toward personality

The book contrasts a social preference for ease, adaptability, and non-exclusive attachment with ideals that emphasize deep, unique, and permanent personal commitments. Cultural expectations make some dispositions comfortable and others difficult; they do not create identical personalities.

## Ordinary cases

Mead uses case material to show variation in household, maturity, intelligence, status, friendship, work, and sexual experience. Avoid summarizing an “average girl” as if it erased individual biographies. Ask what combinations of social support and role fit produce relative ease.

## Conflict cases

Girls experiencing conflict illuminate limits of the general interpretation. Small or isolated households, physical illness, status contradiction, religious schooling, incompatible expectations, family tension, or unusual attachment may concentrate strain. A negative case can refine the mechanism rather than simply invalidate or confirm the whole thesis.

## Adulthood and old age

Youth can be understood only within a life-course structure: how status, labor, sexuality, authority, kin relations, and valued activity change in adulthood and old age. Compare whether adolescence is a sharp identity rupture or one transition in a more continuous role sequence.

## Case matrix

For each person map: biological stage, household, responsibilities, peer position, community status, relationship options, conflicts, health, aspirations, observed conduct, self-account, and later transition.
