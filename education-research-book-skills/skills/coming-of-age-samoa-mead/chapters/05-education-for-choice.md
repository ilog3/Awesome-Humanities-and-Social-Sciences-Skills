# 为选择而教育

## Comparative diagnosis

Mead argues that American adolescents faced many incompatible standards and high-stakes choices while being protected from ordinary realities such as work, birth, death, and varied adult lives. Emotional relationships were often highly exclusive, so a choice could feel like betrayal of a whole person or moral world.

The Samoan comparison is used to show that adolescence need not take the same crisis form. It is not a curriculum to copy wholesale; several described arrangements, including gender separation and collective conformity, may be objectionable or costly.

## Education for choosing

Education should help young people:

- recognize that values and institutions can differ;
- understand consequences and tradeoffs;
- think rather than merely inherit what to think;
- encounter real responsibilities gradually;
- distinguish reversible exploration from irreversible risk;
- tolerate ambiguity without paralysis;
- make commitments without pretending alternatives never existed.

## Design principle

Do not maximize choice indiscriminately. Coordinate:

`meaningful options + accurate knowledge + decision practice + trusted adults + peer support + material feasibility + right to revise`

## Evaluation

Assess decision confidence, reasoning quality, access inequity, stress, help-seeking, ability to revise, and whether young people understand institutional constraints. Avoid interpreting compliance as successful maturation.
