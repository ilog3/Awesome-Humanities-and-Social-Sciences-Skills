# 研究问题与田野

## The question

The study asks whether the distress associated with adolescent girls is an inevitable consequence of puberty or a product of social organization. Rather than study American adolescents alone, Mead uses a cultural contrast to expose assumptions that a single society cannot easily see in itself.

## Field portrait

The book builds its argument through a description of daily life in a small Samoan island community: household organization, status, work, education, peer relations, religion, sexuality, dance, personality judgments, and transitions across the life course. “A Samoan day” provides the ordinary rhythm against which adolescent episodes are interpreted.

## Study design

Mead concentrated on girls at different stages from prepuberty through postpuberty, used observation, interviews and tests, collected household and biographical information, and added broader observations of children and young women. The design was largely cross-sectional rather than a multi-year follow-up of the same individuals.

## Analytic use

Frame any comparison as a mechanism inquiry:

- What options and contradictions face the young person?
- How continuous is the transition into adult roles?
- How are work, sexuality, conflict, death, and responsibility made visible?
- How exclusive are parent-child and romantic attachments?
- How strongly is one permanent identity demanded?

The field portrait is historical. Do not treat it as a description of contemporary Samoa.
