# 方法、争论与证据纪律

## Stated limitations

The methodological appendices acknowledge observer effects, incomplete information across cases, a modest sample, cross-sectional rather than longitudinal inference, translation and recording limits, and the difference between evidence adequate for a general pattern and evidence needed to understand a particular person's conduct.

## Later controversy

Derek Freeman later challenged Mead's language competence, duration, interpretation, attention to violence, and reliance on informants, framing the dispute partly as culture versus biology. Defenders challenged Freeman's sites, timing, interpretation, ethics, and reduction of Mead's position. Treat the controversy as a reason to examine evidence and historical change, not as a binary verdict repeated from authority.

## Audit a claim

1. Locate Mead's exact observation or inference.
2. Identify whether it rests on behavior, interview, test, case history, or comparison.
3. Note sampling, translation, privacy, and access conditions.
4. Seek variation and conflict cases inside the original account.
5. Compare later evidence from the same place and different historical periods.
6. Separate biological maturation from culturally organized meaning and response.
7. State which conclusion survives, weakens, or becomes undecidable.

## Ethical-historical caution

Terms such as “primitive” reflect the period and should not be adopted as current classification. Protect the dignity and contemporaneity of studied people. The book is both a field account and a 1920s intervention in American debates; its rhetorical contrast shaped what it highlighted.
