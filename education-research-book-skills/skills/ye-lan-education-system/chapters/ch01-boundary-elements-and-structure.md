# Boundary, Elements, and Structure

## Define before explaining

Begin with the activity's direct object and aim. An event can affect development without being organized as education. Conversely, an activity organized as education can have negative or unintended effects. Keep classification separate from evaluation.

The book's working definition treats education as conscious social activity directly aimed at influencing human physical and psychological development. School education is a specialized, strongly organized subset rather than the whole of education.

## Four-element map

| Element | Questions |
|---|---|
| Educator | Who accepts responsibility for teaching or educational influence? What authority, knowledge, motive, and constraints do they have? |
| Learner | Who undertakes learning? What prior development, needs, interpretations, choices, and capacities shape participation? |
| Content | What shared object is selected, structured, interpreted, practiced, and evaluated? Whose knowledge and values does it carry? |
| Material conditions | Which spaces, time, tools, media, bodies, technologies, finance, and infrastructure enter the process? What is the minimum viable condition? |

Do not let attractive technology obscure weak relations or teaching. Material conditions enable and constrain but do not automatically determine the activity's quality.

## Compound subject

Analyze education as interwoven teaching and learning:

- in teaching, the educator studies the learner and organizes content and conditions;
- in learning, the learner acts on content and interprets the educator and environment;
- both are active participants relative to the shared content and educational process;
- their authority and responsibility are not identical;
- roles can partly shift through peer learning, self-education, and reciprocal learning.

Avoid both educator-only and learner-only accounts. Ask how each party's action conditions the other's next move.

## School and non-school structure

School education and non-school education—family, community, associations, media, workplaces, and self-directed learning—interact. Determine whether they complement, contradict, duplicate, or remain disconnected. In contemporary analysis, include digital platforms and informal online learning.

## Four operational layers

1. **System layer:** public purposes, law, financing, institutional types, curriculum frameworks, and long-range planning.
2. **Institution layer:** school mission, organization, activity structure, staffing, climate, and feedback.
3. **Educator-design layer:** interpretation of aims, study of learners, curriculum and activity design, and responsive adjustment.
4. **Learner-activity layer:** participation through which external purposes and content may become needs, capabilities, identity, and development.

The pathway is not a command chain. Record downward specification, implementation gaps, learner response, local adaptation, upward feedback, and cross-level effects.

## System properties

Expect wholeness, hierarchy, openness, interaction with environment, internal differentiation, feedback, dynamic balance, and probability rather than mechanical certainty. Educational control combines external regulation with the self-regulation of educators and learners. Design feedback that increases informed self-direction rather than mere surveillance.
