# Lifespan and Education

## Use stages carefully

Life stages help educators notice changing bodily, cognitive, social, emotional, practical, and identity-related tasks. They do not provide a universal timetable or permission to ignore individual and cultural variation.

For any age group:

1. establish current capability and lived context;
2. identify likely transitions and demands;
3. distinguish evidence-based regularities from historical norms;
4. investigate within-group variation;
5. select activity, content, support, and responsibility that fit the learner;
6. protect continuity across transitions;
7. review actual response instead of assuming stage fit.

## Continuity and distinctiveness

Each period inherits earlier development, has present value, and prepares possibilities for later life. Avoid treating childhood only as preparation for adulthood, adolescence only as instability, adulthood as completed development, or later life as decline. Current research and the learner's situation should guide application.

## Three educational tasks

The book describes education as contributing to:

- **formation:** supporting capabilities and qualities not yet developed;
- **correction:** changing a trajectory or practice that impedes development;
- **compensation:** supplying missing opportunity, experience, condition, or support.

Modernize these terms. Locate barriers in environments and institutions as well as individuals. “Correction” must not mean normalization, punishment, identity suppression, or coercive treatment. “Compensation” should usually be framed as accessibility, opportunity, restoration, or support.

## Transition audit

At school entry, phase change, graduation, employment, family change, adult study, or later-life learning, ask:

- What old demands end and new demands begin?
- Which relationships, roles, and identities change?
- What prior capabilities transfer?
- Which environmental supports disappear?
- What voice and choice does the learner have?
- Who must coordinate continuity?

Use multiple evidence sources and revisable plans; development is probabilistic, not mechanically produced by age or instruction.
