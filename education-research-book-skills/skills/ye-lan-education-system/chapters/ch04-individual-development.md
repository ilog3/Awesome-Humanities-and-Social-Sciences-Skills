# Individual Development: From Possibility to Reality

## Development concept

Treat individual development as change in physical and psychological dimensions and in their integrated structure and qualities across the lifespan. It is orderly without being uniform, socially situated without being mechanically determined, and shaped by the person's active relation with the environment.

## Two classes of factor

The book distinguishes:

- **possibility factors:** the learner's existing bodily and psychological conditions, prior development, and environmental conditions;
- **actualizing factors:** the learner's own activity through which person and environment interact and possibilities become actual development.

This distinction prevents the simple addition of heredity plus environment from masquerading as explanation. A resource can be present but unused; a constraint can be interpreted, resisted, or partly transformed.

## Development-pathway analysis

1. Define the developmental change of interest without reducing it to a score.
2. Describe prior development, health, experience, motivation, knowledge, identity, and self-regulation.
3. Describe material, relational, cultural, institutional, and digital environments.
4. Identify the activity in which the learner actually encounters and acts on these conditions.
5. Analyze the activity's goal, role, structure, challenge, meaning, feedback, success, and emotional experience.
6. Trace how repeated participation changes capability, disposition, relation, or identity.
7. Consider alternative pathways and unintended effects.

## School education's special role

Schooling can exert sustained, deliberate, organized, and socially authorized influence. Its role becomes developmental only when system purposes are translated into learner activity and when learners actively appropriate, question, practice, and reconstruct content.

Do not call schooling the sole or automatically dominant cause. Examine family, peers, community, media, work, historical events, and self-directed activity. State the evidence needed to estimate schooling's contribution.

## Intervention logic

Match action to mechanism:

- missing opportunity → change access or environment;
- inaccessible participation → provide accommodation and redesign activity;
- low meaning or agency → reconnect activity with intelligible purposes and choice;
- insufficient prior knowledge → scaffold and sequence;
- harmful relation or feedback → repair climate and interaction;
- structural barrier → act at institution or system level rather than correcting the learner.

Reject deterministic accounts based on heredity, family background, age, disability, class, gender, ethnicity, or a single assessment.
