# Ye Lan's Education-System Analysis

Study an educational problem through **whole → parts → reconstructed whole**. Treat education neither as a classroom-only technique nor as a passive reflection of society. Trace how an open, layered system transforms social purposes into learners' actual development—and how feedback and agency alter that transformation.

## Conceptual core

### Working boundary of education

For this framework, education is a conscious social activity whose direct aim is to influence human physical and psychological development. Distinguish:

- an **educational activity**, organized around that direct aim;
- an **educational influence**, which may arise from other activities without that being their direct aim;
- **school education**, undertaken by specialized institutions and personnel with comparatively strong purpose, organization, sequence, and system.

Use the definition to establish an analytic boundary, not to declare every influence educational or every intended influence beneficial.

### Activity system

Map four basic elements: educator, learner, educational content, and educational material conditions or media.

Educator and learner form a **compound subject**: their responsibilities differ, yet teaching cannot replace learning and learning does not erase the educator's active work. Each interprets and changes the other's activity through feedback. Read [chapters/ch01-boundary-elements-and-structure.md](chapters/ch01-boundary-elements-and-structure.md).

### Layered transformation

Trace the route:

`social needs → system-level design → school-level design → educator design → learner activity and development`

Analyze macro, meso, and micro layers without collapsing one into another. Look for both downward specification and upward or cross-level feedback.

### Two inseparable functions

Evaluate education's contribution to:

1. social continuity, transformation, and development;
2. each learner's physical, intellectual, moral, practical, creative, and personal development.

The first must ultimately operate through real people; the second always occurs in a social world. Reject one-sided optimization.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the object.** State whether the case concerns an educational activity, an educational influence, a school, or a wider education system.
2. **Map the activity.** Specify the four elements, the teaching–learning relationship, missing conditions, and feedback loops.
3. **Locate system layers.** Identify decisions at system, institution, educator, and learner levels; mark where an aim is translated, distorted, resisted, or renewed.
4. **Place the case historically.** Ask how the arrangement emerged and which claims depend on a particular time and social order. Read [chapters/ch02-historical-system-change.md](chapters/ch02-historical-system-change.md).
5. **Analyze the social relations.** Examine population, material production, politics, and culture separately before reconstructing their interaction. Read [chapters/ch03-education-and-social-subsystems.md](chapters/ch03-education-and-social-subsystems.md).
6. **Model development.** Separate potential conditions from the learner's activity that converts possibilities into actual development. Read [chapters/ch04-individual-development.md](chapters/ch04-individual-development.md).
7. **Check life-stage fit.** Use developmental timing as a hypothesis and planning aid, never a rigid norm or destiny. Read [chapters/ch05-lifespan-and-education.md](chapters/ch05-lifespan-and-education.md).
8. **Audit both functions.** At each layer, test social purposes and individual development, including unintended effects and function displacement. Read [chapters/ch06-social-and-individual-functions.md](chapters/ch06-social-and-individual-functions.md).
9. **Design and evaluate.** Propose changes at the levels that control the relevant conditions; use near-, medium-, and long-horizon evidence because educational effects may be delayed.

## Required output

Produce an analytic boundary and unit of analysis; system map and four elements; layer-by-layer transformation and feedback; historical and current context; social-subsystem analysis; potential-to-actual development pathway; dual-function audit; leverage points and responsible actors; multi-horizon indicators; risks; and current evidence or policy still needing verification.

## Guardrails

- Do not infer educational quality from stated intention; examine activity, experience, and effects.
- Do not treat the learner as a passive object or romanticize learner agency as independence from conditions and teaching.
- Do not reduce education to economic productivity, political compliance, examination results, or individual preference.
- Do not use one system level's logic as a substitute for another's work.
- Do not attribute outcomes to heredity, environment, age, or schooling alone; trace interaction and learner activity.
- Do not treat 1991 statistics, institutions, terminology, or political and demographic propositions as current without verification.
- Do not treat developmental averages as universal stages, or older disability and difference labels as acceptable current categories.
- Keep empirical claims, value judgments, causal explanations, and policy choices visibly distinct.
