## Core framework

Read each life through four linked scales:

`family and early schooling ↔ educator's commitments ↔ university trajectory ↔ national/international change`

Trace continuity and revision across three periods: before 1949; socialist construction and political campaigns from 1949 to 1978; and reconstruction, reform, and international exchange after 1978.

Do not use biography merely as illustration after deciding the theory. Let specific lives test broad claims about modernization, revolution, cultural transfer, and institutional change.

## Working procedure

1. Name the person, institution, period, and analytical question.
2. Build a chronological evidence line before explaining motives or continuity.
3. Connect personal choices to institutional opportunities and constraints.
4. Identify traditions inherited, rejected, or recombined: Chinese classical thought, American progressivism and liberal education, European influences, Soviet organization, or later global exchange.
5. Compare at least two lives or universities before claiming a feature of “Chinese education.”
6. Separate retrospective interpretation from independently established historical fact.
7. End with counterevidence, silences, and sample limits.

## Recurring conclusions

- Foreign models were selected, resisted, and recombined rather than simply copied.
- Personal continuity can persist through political discontinuity, while later narration may make a life appear more coherent than it felt.
- Universities have institutional personalities shaped by region, discipline, historical memory, and leadership.
- The six educators evaluate foreign and Chinese traditions differently, yet commonly emphasize human development and teacher/student quality.
- Narrative reveals meanings obscured by macro-level institutional history.

## Evidence discipline

- Attribute claims to these six narrated lives and the author's interpretation.
- Avoid hero biography: include compromise, interruption, conflict, and constraint.
- Do not treat six senior scholars as representative of all educators or institutions.
- Note that long-term trust enabled depth while influencing selection and interpretation.
- Verify dates or present-day institutional claims with current sources when accuracy matters.

## Preferred output

Return: **question → timeline → person/institution link → cultural influences → continuity and rupture → comparison → limits**. Distinguish source interpretation from your own extension.
